The Biology of Cancer

Notes on The Biology of Cancer by Robert A. Weinberg, third edition (2023).

Chapter 1: Cells and Genes
How genes make proteins, and why each cell has two copies of every gene. How mutations arise when cells divide, and how a mutation marks all the descendants of one cell. How DNA methylation can switch a gene off without changing its sequence.

Chapter 2: Nature of Cancer
What a tumor is, how tumors are named after the tissue they come from, and the stages from normal tissue to invasive cancer. Why each tumor grows from a single cell. Why cancer rates differ between countries, what tobacco and other exposures do, and how carcinogens damage DNA.

Chapter 3: Tumor Viruses
How a chicken virus causes cancer, and how it changes cells in a dish. How viruses insert their genes into chromosomes. How the cancer gene of this virus turned out to be a normal gene of the chicken, and which infections cause about one-fifth of human cancers.

Chapter 4: Cellular Oncogenes
How researchers found cancer genes in human tumors that have no virus. Why these are the same genes that the viruses had captured. The two ways a normal gene becomes a cancer gene: its protein changes, as in the ras gene of a bladder cancer, or the cell makes too much of a normal protein, as with the myc gene.

Chapter 5: Growth Factors
Why a normal cell divides only when other cells send it growth factors and when it is attached. How the receptors for growth factors send signals into the cell, and how mutant receptors send signals without any growth factor. How the Ras protein switches itself off, and why mutant Ras cannot.

Chapter 6: Signaling Pathways
How the signal of a receptor travels to the nucleus, through Ras and three branches of proteins. Which parts of these pathways are broken in tumors. How feedback switches signals off, and why a drug that blocks one protein works in some cancers and not in others.

Chapter 7: Tumor Suppressors
Genes that protect cells from becoming cancer cells. A cell loses this protection only when both copies of such a gene are lost. What the eye tumor retinoblastoma shows about this, how the second copy is usually lost, and how such genes were found. How the loss of the gene APC starts most colon cancers.

Chapter 8: Cell Cycle Control
The phases of the cell cycle, and the point late in the first phase after which a cell divides without further growth factors. How the proteins cyclin, CDK and pRb control this point, and the many ways tumors disable this control.

Chapter 9: p53 and Apoptosis
The p53 protein detects damage inside the cell, and its gene is the one mutated most often in human cancer. How the cell keeps p53 low until it is needed, and why one mutant copy disables most of it. How apoptosis, the suicide program of the cell, works, and how tumors escape it.

Chapter 10: Cell Immortality
Why normal cells can divide only a limited number of times, and why this limit stops most tumors. How the ends of the chromosomes, the telomeres, get shorter at each division, what happens when they become too short, and how cancer cells keep them long.

Chapter 11: Multi-Step Tumorigenesis
Why most cancers appear late in life, after several rare changes. How colon cancer passes through visible stages, each with new mutations, and how each mutant clone replaces the one before it. Why one oncogene cannot transform a normal cell, and how tumor promoters, hormones and inflammation speed up the steps.

Chapter 12: Genome Instability
How cells protect their DNA from errors and damage, and how cancer cells lose this protection. Why stem cells divide rarely, how copying errors are corrected, and which chemicals and radiations damage DNA. The repair systems, the inherited diseases that disable them, and how a repair defect can be used to kill cancer cells.

Chapter 13: Stroma and Angiogenesis
The normal cells that live inside a tumor and the signals they exchange with the cancer cells. How tumors use the program of wound healing, why a tumor cannot grow beyond a fraction of a millimeter without new blood vessels, how it switches on their growth, and what drugs that block this growth can do.

Chapter 14: Invasion and Metastasis
How cancer cells leave a tumor and grow elsewhere, which is what kills most patients. The steps from local invasion to a growing metastasis, the change of state that makes a cell mobile and the factors that control it, why almost no travelling cell succeeds, cells that wait for years, and metastases in bone.
