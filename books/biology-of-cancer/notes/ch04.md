# Ch 4 Cellular Oncogenes (book p.101-123; raw lines 15196-17990)

GIST: Human tumors carry mutant versions of the same proto-oncogenes the retroviruses stole, created by somatic mutation
(no virus). Transfection of tumor DNA into NIH 3T3 cells finds them. Two routes of activation:
(1) STRUCTURE of the protein changes: H-ras point mutation G12V (one base out of 3.2 billion), truncated EGF receptor, Bcr-Abl fusion;
(2) EXPRESSION LEVEL deregulated: myc amplified (HSR/DM), myc translocated next to immunoglobulin enhancers (Burkitt), provirus insertion.
Every route removes normal control; the result is a gene that pushes growth constantly.

1. 1970s: viruses not found in most human tumors (only hepatomas and cervical carcinomas tied to viruses in West). Endogenous retrovirus theory failed.
   Theory left: carcinogens mutate growth-controlling genes (proto-oncogenes) in place.
2. Transfection (1972 calcium phosphate co-precipitation). Recipient NIH 3T3 mouse fibroblasts. Donor: C3H10T1/2 mouse cells transformed by 3-methylcholanthrene (no virus). 1978-79: donor DNA -> foci of transformed, anchorage-independent, tumorigenic cells; normal DNA -> no foci.
   Single gene: each recipient takes up only ~0.1% of a genome of donor DNA -> probability of two unlinked genes together = 10^-3 x 10^-3 = 10^-6 -> one gene transforms.
   Human bladder (EJ/T24), lung, colon carcinoma, promyelocytic leukemia DNAs also transform mouse cells -> oncogenes act across species and tissues.
3. Same genes as retroviruses: Southern blot with Harvey virus H-ras probe detects the bladder carcinoma oncogene; K-ras probe detects colon carcinoma oncogene (Parada 1982). Table 4.1: abl (CML), erbB (gastric, lung, breast), fms (AML), kit (GIST), raf (bladder), sis/PDGF, H-ras, K-ras, erbA, ets, myc, rel.
   Amplification: myc 10-20 copies/diploid genome in HL-60 leukemia. erbB amplified in stomach, breast, brain tumors.
   HER2/neu/erbB2 amplified in breast cancers (Slamon 1987): >5 copies -> relapse more likely (Kaplan-Meier). Median survival 6-7 years (normal HER2 protein) vs 3 years (elevated) (Slamon 1989).
   2010: 77 genes recurrently amplified and likely causal (driver vs passenger co-amplified neighbors). Amplicon typically 0.5-10 Mb.
   Only 40-60% of amplified genes show increased RNA.
   Irony: c-src itself almost never mutated in human tumors.
4. H-ras point mutation (1982, three labs simultaneously; Tabin et al. Nature 300:143): bladder carcinoma oncogene and normal proto-oncogene on same 6.6 kb fragment, identical restriction maps; recombining segments localized difference to a 350 bp segment; sequencing: single G->T at codon 12, GGC (Gly) -> GTC (Val). First specific mutation causing a human cancer; somatic.
   Story: smoker 40 years, carcinogens excreted in urine, one urothelial cell mutated.
   Mutations cluster at codons 12, 13 (12% as often as 12), 61 of the 189-codon reading frame (~10,000 K-ras sequences, COSMIC) -> strong selection; other codons neutral or harmful.
   40,000 tumors: K-ras 22%, N-ras 8.2%, H-ras 3.7% activating mutations.
   Table 4.2 % tumors with point-mutated ras: pancreas 98 (K); thyroid papillary 60; follicular 55; colorectal 52 (K,N); seminoma 45; myeloma 43; NSCLC 32 (K); melanoma 30 (N); uterine endometrioid 25; AML 11 (N); bladder 10.
   Knock-in mice: H-ras reading frame in K-ras locus -> carcinogen-induced lung tumors carry H-ras -> regulatory sequences decide which ras gets hit.
   Both mechanisms combine: Harvey virus H-ras is point mutated AND overexpressed; mutant ras often later amplified.
5. myc: 3 more routes, all = deregulated high constitutive expression:
   - amplification: N-myc amplified in ~30% of advanced neuroblastomas -> poor prognosis (Kaplan-Meier event-free survival, Schmidt 2000); HSRs or DMs.
   - insertional mutagenesis (ALV).
   - translocation: Burkitt's lymphoma (East/Central Africa children; EBV + malaria). t(8;14) most common: myc on chr 8 placed next to IgH enhancer (chr 14); also kappa (chr 2) and lambda (chr 22) light chains. Frequencies: kappa 9%, lambda 16%, heavy 75%. Antibody-gene rearranging enzymes misfire. Leder 1983.
   - >300 recurring translocations catalogued; >100 hybrid genes cloned. ~70% of prostate carcinomas: translocations deregulating ERG or ETV1 (2005).
   - HMGA2 translocation removes 7 let-7 miRNA sites in its 3'UTR -> escape repression.
6. Structural changes: EGF receptor truncated (lacks most extracellular domain) in ~1/3 of glioblastomas -> signals without EGF. ~60 similar receptors.
   Bcr-Abl: t(9;22)(q34;q11), Philadelphia chromosome, >95% of CML; Abl tyrosine kinase fused to Bcr -> deregulated firing. MLL fuses with >=50 partners. PAX3/7-FKHR in 80% alveolar rhabdomyosarcomas. EML4-ALK in NSCLC. Table 4.5.
7. Infections: ~1/5 of cancer deaths worldwide. Stomach cancer 9% of cancer mortality (H. pylori); liver 6% (HBV/HCV); cervix 5% (HPV). Table 4.6 viruses: EBV, HTLV-I, HHV8, HBV, HCV, HPV, JCV, MCV (Merkel cell). Viral oncogenes are ancient, serve viral replication; cancer a side effect.
