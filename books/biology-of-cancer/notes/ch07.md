# Ch 7 Tumor Suppressor Genes (book p.233-273; raw 31643-36484)

GIST: Every accelerator has a brake. Tumor suppressor genes (TSGs) restrain growth; cancer needs BOTH copies lost (recessive at the
cell level). Retinoblastoma: Knudson 1971 - familial (inherited one bad copy) tumors arise with one-hit kinetics, bilateral, multiple;
sporadic need two hits, unilateral, single. The second hit is usually NOT an independent mutation (1e-6) but loss of heterozygosity
(LOH: mitotic recombination 1e-5..1e-4, gene conversion, nondisjunction, deletion) or promoter methylation. LOH maps TSGs.
Inherited cancer syndromes are almost always TSG (recessive in cell, dominant in pedigree) because dominant oncogenes in germ line disrupt development.

1. Cell fusion (Oxford): normal x tumor hybrids non-tumorigenic -> cancer phenotype recessive; exception: virus-transformed cells stay tumorigenic (viral oncogenes dominant).
2. Argument for TSGs: inactivating a gene is easy (many sites, frameshifts, deletions) vs activating (ras only codons 12,13,61). Argument against: two hits needed; 1e-6 per copy -> 1e-12 per cell generation.
3. Retinoblastoma: ~1 in 20,000 children; retinal cone precursors; diagnosed birth to 6-8 years.
   Sporadic: unilateral, single tumor, cured -> no further risk. Familial: bilateral, multiple tumors; >500x risk of osteosarcoma in adolescence; half of offspring affected.
   Fig 7.3A: 1601 patients 1914-1984 (Kleinerman 2005): bilateral much higher risk of later tumors.
   Knudson 1971 (PNAS 68:820): bilateral = one-hit kinetics, unilateral = two-hit kinetics (Fig 7.4; need image for data).
   Sidebar 7.1: dominant in pedigree, recessive in cell: retina ~5x10^6 susceptible cells x LOH rate 1e-6 -> ~5 tumors per eye.
   Some bilateral with no family history: de novo germ-line mutation (>80% arise in father's spermatogenesis for NF1; same for Rb).
4. LOH mechanisms: mitotic recombination (1e-5 to 1e-4 per cell generation), gene conversion (even more frequent), deletion (hemizygosity), nondisjunction (lose chromosome, then duplicate remaining). Colon carcinoma LOH often via translocations.
5. Rb located: 1978 interstitial deletions shared 13q14; esterase D (13q14, two electrophoretic alleles) lost in tumors of heterozygous children -> LOH. Anonymous markers (AAGCC/AAGTC) become homozygous. 1986 Rb cloned (Friend et al. Nature 323:643); ~190 kb, homozygous deletions in tumors; osteosarcoma deletion inside gene. Why retina and bone? Rb loss sufficient in retinal precursors; elsewhere multi-step. Rb+/- mice get pituitary & thyroid tumors, not retinoblastoma.
6. Finding TSGs by LOH: RFLPs (EcoRI site present/absent); Vogelstein 1989 colon cancers: 17p and 18q high LOH vs background 15-20% for other arms (8p also). SNPs (>100 million; common one every few kb heterozygous) -> finer mapping. CDKN2A/CDKN2B homozygous deletions at 9p21 in 80 cell lines. >30 TSGs cloned by LOH; now >=200 TSGs. Table 7.1 (Rb 13q14, TP53 17p13 Li-Fraumeni, APC 5q21 FAP, NF1 17q11, PTEN 10q23 Cowden, VHL 3p25, p16INK4A 9p21 familial melanoma, BRCA not listed here..., DPC4/Smad4 18q21 juvenile polyposis, KEAP1, LKB1 Peutz-Jeghers, NF2, WT1 Wilms, CDH1 familial gastric, PTC Gorlin, TSC1/2, miR-15a/16-1...).
7. Promoter methylation: CpG islands; 60-70% of genes have CpG island promoters (12,000-14,000 genes vulnerable). Cancer: global hypomethylation (repeats) + focal CpG island hypermethylation. >half of TSGs mutated in familial syndromes are silenced by methylation in sporadic cancers. CIMP: up to 5% of genes (~1000). Table 7.2 hypermethylated genes (BRCA1, p16, APC, MLH1, PTEN, VHL, RB, MGMT, CDH1...). p16INK4A methylated in 44% of normal-looking lung epithelial cells of current/former smokers, 0% of never smokers; LOH 71-73% smokers vs 1.5-1.7% nonsmokers -> early, before histology changes. Normal breast lobules with uniform p16 methylation.
8. Only shared property of TSGs: anti-cancer function. Gatekeepers (control proliferation/differentiation/death) vs caretakers (genome maintenance, ch12).
9. NF1: neurofibromatosis type 1, 1 in 3500; neurofibromas, cafe au lait spots, Lisch nodules; MPNSTs, gliomas, pheochromocytomas, myeloid leukemias. Variable expressivity. Cloned 1990. NF1 = Ras-GAP (arginine finger R1276; >1000x GTPase stimulation; mutation reduces 1000x). Nf1-/- myeloid cells: higher basal Ras-GTP, 3x further rise with GM-CSF, sustained. Neurofibroma needs Nf1-/- Schwann cells AND Nf1+/- mast cells (haploinsufficient) (Yang 2008). 13 Ras-GAPs; RASA1, RASA2, DAB2IP, RASAL2 lost in cancers.
   Haploinsufficiency: Smad4+/- polyps, p27+/- tumors, PTEN+/- prostate acceleration, Dmp1+/-.
10. APC & colonic crypt: >95% of colon cancers sporadic; FAP <1% of colon cancers, hundreds-thousands of polyps; Apc cloned 1991 (Utah Mormon pedigrees, chromosome 5q). Apc mutated in ~90% of sporadic colon cancers. APC protein 2843 aa; with axin, GSK-3b phosphorylates b-catenin -> ubiquitin-proteasome. Mutations truncate b-catenin/axin binding regions (mutation cluster region).
    Crypt: Wnt from stroma (colon) / Paneth cells (small intestine) at bottom -> high b-catenin -> stem cells; daughters migrate up, b-catenin falls, differentiate, die and shed in 3-4 days. Defense: mutated cells are discarded within days. Only a mutation that blocks exit (APC loss, b-catenin stabilizing mutation) lets mutant cells stay and accumulate more hits. APC loss comes FIRST in colon cancer; K-ras, p53 later in variable order.
    Of ~10% colon cancers with wild-type APC: APC methylated or b-catenin mutated (GSK-3b sites) -> same result, mutually exclusive.
    Tcf4-/- mice: no crypt stem cells. Min mouse (chemically induced Apc point mutation): ~100 small-intestine polyps; LOH in tumors.
    Apc loss in stem cells -> adenomas in weeks; in transit-amplifying cells -> microadenomas fizzle. shRNA Apc off -> tumors; restore Apc -> rapid regression even of advanced tumors.
11. KEAP1-NRF2: KEAP1 degrades NRF2 (half-life 20 min); ROS/electrophiles modify KEAP1 cysteines -> NRF2 to nucleus, ARE genes (antioxidants, detox). Normally protective (prevents initiation) but in tumors helps survive oxidative stress & chemotherapy. KEAP1 mutated in half of lung cancer lines; lung adenocarcinoma 17%, squamous 12%; NRF2 gain-of-function mutations at KEAP1-binding site; mutually exclusive. Nrf2 loss enhances initiation by carcinogens but reduces progression.
12. Germ-line oncogene syndromes rare: Costello (H-ras), familial GIST (Kit, PDGFRA), hereditary papillary renal (Met), MEN2 (Ret), Noonan (PTPN11, K-ras, SOS), familial neuroblastoma (ALK). Activated K-ras sperm -> embryos die mid-gestation; germline alleles weaker.
    Why a TSG+/- carrier (1e13 cells, LOH 1e-4..1e-5) doesn't get millions of tumors: most cancers are multi-step; TSG loss necessary not sufficient; retinoblastoma is exception.
13. Criteria for a TSG: LOH in many tumors + inactivating mutations on remaining allele; methylation; mouse knockouts; biochemical rationale. "The very existence of a TSG becomes apparent only when it is absent." Therapy: gene replacement impractical (delivery); target pathways activated by loss (mTOR inhibitors in PTEN-null tumors); synthetic lethal screens.
