# Ch 14 Moving Out: Invasion and Metastasis (book p.609-695; raw 80062-88801)

GIST: Metastases cause ~90% of cancer deaths. The invasion-metastasis cascade: local invasion (breach basement membrane)
-> intravasation -> transport in blood/lymph -> arrest (physical trapping in capillaries 3-8 um vs cancer cells >20 um) ->
extravasation -> micrometastasis -> colonization. Physical dissemination is fairly efficient; colonization is extremely
inefficient (1 in 1000 marrow DTCs -> micromet; 1 in 1000 micromets -> macromet). No metastasis-specific mutations found:
invasiveness comes from an epigenetic program, the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), borrowed from gastrulation,
neural crest and wound healing, run by embryonic TFs (Snail, Slug, Twist, Zeb1/2, FOXC2, Goosecoid, Prrx1) and switched on
by stromal signals (TGF-b, Wnts, TNF-a, EGF/HGF, stiff ECM, PGE2) at the tumor-stroma edge; self-sustained by autocrine
loops; partial EMT (hybrid E/M) cells are the stem-like tumor-initiating cells; EMT must reverse (MET) at the distant site
for colonies to grow. Colonization needs organ-specific adaptation (Paget 1889 "seed and soil"): e.g., breast cancer in bone
runs the "vicious cycle" PTHrP -> osteoblast RANKL -> osteoclasts dissolve bone -> TGF-b, IGF-1, Ca2+ released -> more PTHrP
(drugs: bisphosphonates, denosumab). Dormant micrometastases can erupt decades later.

Intro: 1 cm^3 ~10^9 cells; tumors reach 10^10-10^11 cells before detection. Primary tumors ~10% of cancer deaths, metastases ~90%.
  Breast -> brain, liver, bone, lung; prostate -> bone; colon -> liver. Melanoma past a depth ~certain metastasis; basal cell carcinoma,
  astrocytoma rarely. Metastasis-specific mutation searches consistently failed.
14.1 Carcinomas >80% of life-threatening cancers. Cascade (Fidler): 7 steps. Loss of BM in T3 rectal carcinomas predicts distant
  metastasis (Spaderna 2006). Single-cell vs collective invasion (leader cells basal K14+, partial EMT). Proteases from stroma
  (fibroblasts lead SCC cells; macrophage cathepsins in Rip-Tag).
14.2 EMT: epithelial -> mesenchymal (E-cadherin, cytokeratin down; vimentin, fibronectin, N-cadherin up; MMP-2/9; motility,
  invasiveness, apoptosis/radio resistance, stem traits). Embryo EMTs (Table 14.1): gastrulation, heart valves, neural crest, somites,
  palate, Mullerian duct. E-cadherin keystone: loss alone mimics EMT; 26 breast cancer lines: 8 inactivated E-cadherin expression, 5 truncating,
  3 in-frame deletions; germ-line CDH1 -> familial gastric cancer; re-expression suppresses invasion. N-cadherin binds stroma (melanoma
  E->N switch). Partial EMT spectrum (energy landscape).
14.3 Contextual signals: Oft 1996: EpRas + TGF-b -> EMT; autocrine TGF-b maintains; blocking reverts (MET). TGF-b switches from cytostatic
  to pro-invasive after ras/Rb changes; high TGF-b = poor prognosis. EMT at tumor edges touching stroma (vimentin, avb6 integrin, MMP-2).
  Stiff collagen + TGF-b -> Snail+ invasive fingers. Table 14.3 signals: TGF-b, Wnts, Notch, RTKs (EGFR, FGFR, Met), cytokines, stiff ECM,
  absence of epithelial neighbors, periostin, collagen I. Scheel 2011: autocrine TGF-b + canonical + non-canonical Wnt maintain the
  mesenchymal state; epithelial cells secrete inhibitors (DKK1, SFRP1, BMPs). Set points per cell type. Sidebar 14.1 MET at metastases:
  PyMT lung colonization: GFP+ cells 0.1% -> 0.03% -> grow; mesenchymal CD90+CD24+ fraction 1.5% -> >20% in first week -> back to ~1.8%;
  Twist1 kept on blocks colonization, off -> 12 of 14 mice metastases (Tsai 2012).
14.4 EMT-TFs: Snail/SNAI1, Slug/SNAI2, Twist, Goosecoid, FOXC2, Zeb1, Zeb2, E47, Prrx1; ancient (sea anemone >600 My). Snail, Slug, Zeb1,
  Zeb2 bind CDH1 promoter. Zeb1/2 vs miR-200 double-negative feedback = bistable switch; NCI-60 lines sit at E or M ends (Park 2008).
14.5 Stroma: CAFs boost invasion of CRC spheroids; PyMT Csf1 op/op: no TAMs -> primary grows normally but stays encapsulated, no lung
  metastases (Lin 2001). Paracrine loop: macrophage EGF -> cancer cell CSF-1 -> macrophages (Wyckoff 2004). TMEM triads (tumor cell +
  macrophage + endothelial cell, VEGF-A opens doorways): ER+/HER2- breast cancers (~60% of breast tumors), top third triad density 2.7x
  distant metastasis risk vs bottom third; better than grade (Robinson 2009).
14.6 EMT -> stemness: Mani 2008: Twist/Snail push HMLE cells into CD44hi CD24lo (stem) state; natural CD44hi CD24lo cells: E-cadherin mRNA
  ~1/100, N-cadherin, vimentin, fibronectin 65-280x, EMT-TFs 9-200x. Ye 2015: Snail-high tumor cells TIC 1/143 vs Snail-low 1/46,014
  (>300x). Slug in normal basal mammary stem cells; transient Slug + Sox9: 100 cells reconstitute gland vs 10,000 controls (>100x) (Guo 2012).
  Hybrid E/M cells best TICs; p63 needed for metastasis seeding.
14.7 Necessity: Twist siRNA 4T1 lung metastases -85% (primary unchanged) (Yang 2004); Slug siRNA (80% knockdown) melanoma metastases -90%;
  Zeb1 shRNA. Snail half-life 25 min (GSK-3b; Akt, TNF-a/NF-kB/CSN2 stabilize). Twist before tail-vein injection: extravasation ~20% -> ~80%
  at 36 h. Transient Slug+Sox9 (2 weeks) in MCF7-ras: invasive margins 10 weeks later; ~400 micro- and ~100 macro-metastases vs ~none.
  Clinic: Snail (breast relapse), Twist (melanoma), Slug (gastric), full EMT (lung adeno) -> worse survival.
14.8 ECM clearance: MMPs mostly from stroma (macrophages raise MMP-2 4-5x). MT1-MMP (membrane) on invadopodia; activates pro-MMP-2;
  cleaves collagen IV/I. BM pores ~40 nm vs nuclei 3-10 um (~100x). MMP inhibitor trials failed (joint stiffness/pain). MMP-3 transgene alone
  -> hyperplasia -> invasive metastatic tumors. TIMPs.
14.9 Motility: HGF/scatter factor (Met), EGF. Lamellipodia, filopodia, focal contacts. EMT alters splicing: MenaINV: with macrophages ~200x
  trans-endothelial migration; responds to 25x lower EGF; more lung metastases.
14.10 Intravasation (TMEM doorways); clumps may be main founders. Lymph nodes: ~1/3 of breast, colorectal, cervical, oral carcinoma
  patients node+ at diagnosis (breast >30%); 90% of long-term survivors had 1-3 positive nodes; <5% of patients with >5 positive nodes
  long-term disease-free. Nodes = surrogate markers; 2/3 of distant-metastasis cells from subclones distinct from node subclones.
  CTCs: shear, NK cells, platelets cloak them; clusters (microemboli) better founders; barcoding: >=80% of metastases oligoclonal.
  Longitudinal CTC counts prognostic (liquid biopsy). Pancreatic cancer: ~15 CTCs/ml portal blood -> ~3x10^7 cells into liver per day,
  yet a handful of metastases. Breast DTCs in marrow: 1/1000 form micrometastasis; 1/1000 micrometastases -> macroscopic (metastatic
  inefficiency). Sidebar 14.5: mouse CTCs <2 min in circulation; capillaries 3-8 um, cancer cells >20 um, RBC ~7 um; >98% cleared within
  10 min in mouse; human half-life ~1-2 h (circulation time ~1 min vs ~4 s mouse). Venous blood -> lungs first; gut/pancreas -> liver (portal).
  Extravasation: Angptl4 (TGF-b induced) opens endothelium; integrin b1 to laminin; or intraluminal growth. Leukocyte diapedesis <1 min;
  cancer cells hours to a day.
14.11 Colonization: Paget 1889 (735 breast cancer patients) seed and soil. Tropism = colonization efficiency + circulation layout.
  Adaptive programs likely epigenetic. >30% of breast cancer patients have marrow micrometastases at diagnosis; only half ever metastatic
  disease. Cytokeratin+ marrow -> ~50% dead within 3 years (Pierga 2004). Naumov 2002: nanoparticle-labeled cells dormant 11 weeks in liver,
  undivided, still tumorigenic; resistant to chemo that shrank growing metastases 75%. Breast relapses 10-20 years later; up to 1/3 of
  treated women have DTCs. Sidebar 14.7: micrometastases heterogeneous early, clonal later -> metastatic shower (secondary wave);
  1986 autopsies of >1500 colon cancer deaths: liver first, then lung, then elsewhere. Pre-metastatic niches: G-CSF -> neutrophilia;
  neutrophil depletion (anti-Ly6G) cuts lung metastases (Coffelt 2015); >12 soluble niche factors by 2016.
14.12 Specialization: papillary thyroid: 75% of young patients node+, only 3% distant; duodenal carcinoids >1 cm (>10^9 cells) node+ but
  rarely liver; peritoneovenous shunts put billions of ascites cancer cells into blood with few metastases. Contralateral breast metastases
  ~2%. Tumor self-seeding (2009). Early dissemination (DTCs before primary is aggressive) - parallel progression debated.
14.13 Bone: ~10% of skeleton replaced per year. Breast -> osteolytic; prostate -> osteoblastic; myeloma purely osteolytic; ~1/4 of breast
  cancer patients also osteoblastic lesions. RANKL (osteoblast) vs OPG decoy. Vicious cycle: PTHrP (normal lactation Ca2+ mobilization) ->
  osteoblast RANKL up, OPG down -> osteoclasts -> TGF-b, IGF-1, Ca2+, BMPs, PDGF, FGFs -> more PTHrP. dn TGF-bRII blocks osteolysis; PTHrP or
  ca TGF-bRI restores (Yin 1999). Bisphosphonates (persist in bone >10 years); zoledronic acid premenopausal +37% overall survival over 7 years
  (2011); denosumab (anti-RANKL) better than bisphosphonates for skeletal events. Hypercalcemia.
14.14 Occult micrometastases: marrow cytokeratin+ in ~1% of non-cancer patients vs 36% of stage I-III breast cancer; 4-year cancer death 25%
  vs 6%; another study >10x risk. Colon (liver resection): alive at 15 months 90% without marrow micromets vs 60% with. Esophageal cancer rib
  marrow 79% and 88%; <half survive 3 years. Kidney transplants from donor with melanoma removed 16 years earlier -> both recipients melanoma.
14.15 Synopsis: cell of origin: Ince 2007: same oncogenes (hTERT, SV40 ER, H-ras) in luminal-like (BPLER) vs myoepithelial-like (HMLER)
  MECs: TIC 1 per 10^2 vs 1 per 10^6 (10^4x), lung metastases 10/11 (fat pad), 9/10 (s.c.) vs 0/8, 0/6. Ramaswamy 2003: 17-gene signature
  from bulk primaries predicts metastasis (breast, prostate, medulloblastoma) -> metastatic ability widespread in primary. Kang 2003: MDA-MB-231
  subclones; 5 genes (IL11, OPN, CTGF, CXCR4, MMP1) high -> bone metastases; 3 of them confer bone tropism. Micrometastases <0.2 mm hold
  hundreds-thousands of cells among ~5x10^13 body cells (another body-cell-count figure!). Primary and metastases transcriptomes similar ->
  same therapies may work. Five experimental difficulties list.
