# Ch 15 Crowd Control: Tumor Immunology (book p.697-735; raw 90871-95481)

GIST: The immune system can tell cancer cells from normal cells because cancer cells display peptides the body was never
tolerized to: neoantigens from mutations (driver or passenger), viral proteins, fusion junctions (Bcr-Abl), and normal proteins
shown in the wrong place or time (cancer-testis MAGE, differentiation antigens, overexpressed HER2). Every nucleated cell shows
chopped-up samples of its own proteins on MHC-I (8-10 aa peptides) to CD8 T cells; dendritic cells also show engulfed material on
MHC-II (13-20 aa) to CD4 helpers, and cross-present on MHC-I to prime CD8 killers in lymph nodes. Activation needs two signals
(TCR + CD28:CD80/86 co-stimulation); brakes: CTLA-4 in the lymph node (outcompetes CD28), PD-1:PD-L1 in the tissue
(exhaustion), Tregs (TGF-b, IL-10, IL-2 sink), M2 macrophages, MDSCs. Evidence of surveillance: immunosuppressed people get far
more virus-driven cancers (Kaposi 3640x in AIDS) but barely more common carcinomas; TIL density predicts survival (CRC relapse
20% vs 80%). Tumors escape by immunoediting (Darwinian loss of immunogenic clones), losing MHC-I/b2m/TAP, secreting TGF-b/IL-10,
CD47 don't-eat-me, PD-L1; NK cells kill MHC-I-low and stressed (NKG2D ligand) cells.

Intro: Burnet & Thomas 1950s immunosurveillance. ~19.3 million new cancer cases worldwide (2021); 1 in 5 US deaths.
15.1 Transplants rejected between inbred strains; syngeneic accepted. 3-MC tumor: irradiated cells immunize against same tumor but not
  against an independently induced tumor -> tumor-specific antigens (Fig 15.1).
15.2 Immunocompromised: germ-line immunodeficiency; ~38 million HIV infected (2019); transplant recipients on immunosuppressants.
  Grulich 2007 meta-analysis SIR (HIV/AIDS; transplant): breast 1.03; 1.15. prostate 0.70; 0.97. colorectal 0.92; 1.69. ovary 1.63; 1.55.
  lung 2.72; 2.18. EBV Hodgkin (likely) 11.03; 3.89. NHL 76.67; 8.07. Kaposi (HHV-8) 3640; 208. Liver (HBV/HCV) 5.22; 2.13 (?).
  Stomach (H. pylori) 1.90 (HIV) (transplant value not in OCR; check figure). -> surveillance mostly vs virus-driven tumors (~20% of cancer
  mortality). Nonviral ~80%: tolerance problem. TILs: ovarian survival (Zhang 2003); melanoma with TILs live 1.5-3x longer; breast, bladder,
  prostate, rectum. Galon 2006: 75 CRC patients, TH1/memory T-cell signature high -> ~20% relapse vs ~80% low; 406 CRC patients stage I-III:
  CD3+ and CD45RO+ high at center and margin -> long survival.
15.3 Innate (germ-line pattern receptors, all phyla, no memory) vs adaptive (vertebrates; >10^7 specificities; memory; vaccination).
  Antibodies (2 heavy + 2 light), BCR (membrane antibody), TCR (heterodimer) recognizes peptide:MHC. Antigen binding necessary but not
  sufficient; immunogen depends on context.
15.4 V(D)J recombination (few hundred segments; ~40 VH, ~25 DH, 6 JH; ~30 Vlambda, ~35 Vkappa); >10^9 specificities each for T and B;
  one receptor per lymphocyte; clonal expansion on antigen (like tumor clonal expansion); autoreactive cells deleted in thymus/marrow =
  tolerance; testis/embryonic antigens escape tolerance. AID somatic hypermutation 100,000x mutation rate in B cells.
15.5 MHC: >10,000 alleles in human population; peptide binding promiscuous; MHC-I on all nucleated cells (own proteins, proteasome, TAP into
  ER); MHC-II on APCs (dendritic cells, B cells, macrophages; engulfed proteins). One amino acid change can make a peptide foreign.
15.6 CD8 = cytotoxic (MHC-I); CD4 = helper (MHC-II): TH1, TH2, TH17, TFH, Treg (5 subsets). CTL kill: perforin + granzymes; FasL; serial killing.
15.7 Dendritic cells carry antigens via lymph to lymph nodes; naive T cells come via blood; cross-presentation (engulfed antigens on MHC-I too).
15.8 Tumor antigens: TSTAs/neoantigens (mutant Ras, p53; Bcr-Abl junction; viral E6/E7); passenger neoantigens can be dropped without cost,
  driver neoantigens cannot. TAAs: differentiation (tyrosinase, PSMA), cancer-testis (MAGE-1/3, NY-ESO), overexpressed (HER2, MUC-1, WT1),
  splicing (NA17). CD20 on B cells and B lymphomas (shielded by tolerance).
15.9 NK cells: kill MHC-I-low cells (KIR, CD94-NKG2A inhibitory); NKG2D senses stress ligands (MICA, Rae1; TRAMP tumors lower Rae1 when
  NKG2D present); IFN-g raises MHC-I; ADCC via Fc-gamma receptors (monoclonal antibody therapy).
15.10 Macrophages M1 (IL-12, kill, TH1) vs M2 (IL-10, TGF-b, VEGF, repair); TH1/CTL IFN-g -> M1; TH2 IL-4/IL-13 -> M2; TGF-b -> M2 and Tregs.
  CD47 don't-eat-me (aging RBCs lose it; tumor cells and breast CTCs raise it). ADCP. Meta-analysis of 55 studies: dense macrophages -> poor survival.
15.11 Tregs: FOXP3+, CD25+; mice/humans without Tregs die of autoimmunity; tumors recruit via CCL22; TGF-b induces; mechanisms: CTLA-4 steals
  CD80/86, IL-2 sink, TGF-b, IL-10. Present in lung, ovarian, breast, pancreatic carcinoma TILs and ascites.
15.12 Checkpoints: naive T cell needs TCR + CD28:CD80/86; CTLA-4 binds CD80/86 more tightly, rises with activation -> shutoff (lymph node);
  ctla4 knockout mice die of lymphoproliferation at 3-4 weeks. PD-1 on activated T cells meets PD-L1/2 in tissue -> inhibition; chronic ->
  exhaustion.
15.13 Synopsis: most incipient tumors may be eliminated unseen; immunoediting selects less immunogenic clones. Escape: antigen loss
  (methylation, deletion; passenger antigens), MHC-I transcriptional loss, b2m loss (5 of 17 MIN CRCs had b2m mutations), TAP1/2 loss (HNSCC);
  NK counter; tumor TGF-b shuts NK; IL-10 (EBV viral IL-10); TAMs, TANs, MDSCs, Bregs; Fas pathway defects; IAPs block granzyme-caspases.
  HER2 in >20% of breast cancers (can't drop it).
