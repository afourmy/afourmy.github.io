# Ch 5 Growth Factors, Receptors, and Cancer (book p.125-169; raw lines 19037-24730)

GIST: A normal cell divides only when its neighbors tell it to (growth factors) and only when it is anchored (integrins).
Oncoproteins fake the "go" signal: the cell behaves as if growth factor were present when none is.
Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) fire when two receptors are brought together (ligand-induced dimerization -> each kinase
phosphorylates the other's tail). Cancer makes them fire without ligand: truncation (v-ErbB, EGF-R in 1/3 glioblastomas),
overexpression (collisions), fusion to a self-dimerizing partner, point mutations; or cancer cells make their own ligand (autocrine, v-sis = PDGF).
Ras is a GTP/GDP switch that turns itself off (GTPase, accelerated 10^5x by GAPs); codon 12/13/61 mutations disable the off switch.

1. Tissues: no cell decides alone; neighbors send growth factors (GFs). In culture, medium with nutrients is not enough: serum required.
   Serum = fluid after blood clots; platelets release PDGF, EGF, TGF-b1, VEGF, IGF, FGF-2 (wound healing). Mitogens. Fibroblasts without PDGF stay alive for weeks but do not divide.
   ECM: >250 structural proteins + up to 3x more associated.
2. Src is a protein kinase (1977-78, Collett & Erikson: immunoprecipitating antibody got phosphorylated). 533 aa, 60 kD (pp60src). >50 substrates -> pleiotropy.
   Src phosphorylates TYROSINE: normal cells >99% phospho-Ser/Thr, phosphotyrosine 0.05-0.1%; v-src cells up to 1%. Ras-transformed cells unchanged. Tyrosine phosphorylation used largely by mitogenic pathways. Kinase-dead Src loses transforming power.
3. EGF receptor: first GF (premature eye opening in newborn mice). Purified from A431 carcinoma line (up to 100x EGF-R). Ectodomain 621 aa, transmembrane 23 aa, cytoplasmic 542 aa with a Src-like tyrosine kinase (sequence homology). Table 5.1 GF/receptor pairs (PDGF, EGF, NGF/Trk, FGF, HGF/Met, VEGF, IGF, GDNF/Ret, SCF/Kit).
   >=58 RTK genes in human genome. RTKs appeared just before metazoa (choanoflagellates have one).
4. 1984: EGF-R sequence ~ v-ErbB (avian erythroblastosis virus). v-ErbB lacks ectodomain -> constitutive, ligand-independent signal. Truncated EGF-R in 1/3 of glioblastomas; truncated HER2 in breast cancer poor prognosis.
   Table 5.2: EGF-R overexpressed in many carcinomas; HER2 overexpressed 30% breast, ~20% gastric; Flt-3 tandem duplication AML; Kit substitutions GIST; Ret fusion/point papillary thyroid, MEN2; FGF-R3 myeloma, bladder...
   Explains reduced serum requirement of cancer cells: signal without GF.
5. sis = PDGF B chain (1983). Simian sarcoma virus cell secretes PDGF-like Sis, which binds its own PDGF-R -> AUTOCRINE loop. PDGF-R on mesenchymal cells (explains why virus transforms fibroblasts not epithelial cells). Normal paracrine: epithelial cells make PDGF for stroma; mesenchymal make TGF-a for epithelia; a cell normally does not display the receptor for the ligand it makes.
   Table 5.3 autocrine tumors (TGF-a/EGF-R in many carcinomas; SCF/Kit; IGF-2; IL-6 myeloma...). Some lung cancers: 3 loops (TGF-a, SCF, IGF-1). Kaposi's sarcoma champion autocrine.
6. Dimerization & transphosphorylation: GF ligands often dimeric; receptors move laterally in membrane; ligand bridges two receptors -> each kinase phosphorylates the other's C-terminal tail ("autophosphorylation" misnomer). Activation loop swings out of catalytic cleft. Phosphotyrosines = docking sites (ch6).
   Overexpressed receptors collide -> ligand-independent dimerization and firing (Met experiment: human Met in mouse liver cannot bind mouse HGF yet causes HCC). TCGA: EGFR amplified in ~90 of 206 glioblastomas (mostly wild-type protein).
   Fusions: FIG-ROS in glioblastoma; partner that dimerizes pulls receptors together.
   HER2 has no known ligand; heterodimerizes with HER1/HER3; HER3 has dead kinase. AXL heterodimers -> drug resistance.
   Negative feedback: phosphatases (103 PTPs), endocytosis & lysosomal degradation (Cbl mono-ubiquitylation). HIP1 overexpression blocks endocytosis -> cells grow in 0.1% serum; GAK knockdown -> 50x surface EGF-R.
   Rat Neu transmembrane Val->Glu point mutation (ENU mutagen) -> constitutive dimer. ~10% NSCLC mutant EGF-R (drug-responsive); >=60% bladder carcinomas altered FGF-R3.
   Germ line: ret -> MEN2A/2B; met -> hereditary papillary renal cancer; Kit (Japanese family GIST, 4 generations). Rare because dominant active alleles disrupt development.
7. Other receptor classes: cytokine receptors with noncovalent JAKs (EPO, TPO, interferon, interleukins); TGF-b receptors (Ser/Thr kinase, type II phosphorylates type I; TGF-b suppresses normal epithelial proliferation but promotes malignancy later); Notch (juxtacrine, ligand-endocytosis pulls ectodomain, proteolysis releases NICD to nucleus; mutant Notch in half of adult T-cell leukemias); Patched-Smoothened (Hedgehog, primary cilium, Gli); Wnt/Frizzled (19 Wnts, 10 Frizzleds; without Wnt GSK-3b phosphorylates b-catenin -> destroyed; with Wnt GSK-3b inhibited, b-catenin to nucleus); GPCRs (>800 genes, ~4%, heterotrimeric G-proteins GDP/GTP; chemokine receptors). Counts: 11 TGFb-like receptors, 4 Notch, 2 Patched, 10 Frizzled.
8. Nuclear receptors (~48): steroid hormones (estrogen, progesterone, androgens) enter cell, bind receptor = transcription factor. Tamoxifen (SERM) shifts helix 12 -> co-repressors; halves relapse in ER+ postmenopausal breast cancer (almost twofold reduction). 55,000 ER studies, 41,000 PR studies (2021).
9. Integrins: 18 alpha x 8 beta -> 24 heterodimers (not 144); bind ECM (collagen, laminin, fibronectin (a5b1)); focal adhesions; link to actin; anti-apoptotic signals; no attachment -> anoikis. Inside-out signaling (motility). b1 integrin knockout in mammary epithelium: >75% fewer hyperplastic nodules with PyMT oncogene, no carcinomas. FAK needed for motility.
   Normal cell proliferation requires TWO conditions: mitogenic GFs AND anchorage. Ras oncogene removes both requirements.
10. Ras: 3 genes, 4 proteins (K-ras splice), ~21 kD, lipid tail (farnesyl etc.) anchors to inner membrane. GTPase switch: GEF swaps GDP->GTP (on), intrinsic GTPase hydrolyzes GTP (off), GAPs accelerate up to 100,000x (arginine finger). Oncogenic Harvey Ras binds GTP but lost GTPase -> stuck ON. Gly12, Gln61 sit next to gamma-phosphate of GTP in catalytic cleft -> substitutions block GAP-stimulated hydrolysis. Switch I/II regions change with GTP.
    Why only 12/13/61: deletions or most point mutations LOSE function; only these gain. Missense, never nonsense. NF1 is a Ras-GAP (loss -> neuroectodermal tumors).
    Ras downstream of RTKs (EGF activates Ras within 5 min near surface), not only upstream via TGF-a autocrine.
    HER2 overexpression -> many phenotypes -> multiple pathways radiate from receptor. Genetics of flies/worms/yeast solved the pathway (ch6).
