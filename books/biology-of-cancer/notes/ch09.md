# Ch 9 p53: Master Guardian and Executioner (book p.323-373; raw 43218-48708)

GIST: pRb watches the outside; p53 watches the inside. Many internal alarms (DNA breaks via ATM/Chk2, single-strand DNA via ATR/Chk1,
oncogene overdrive via E2F->ARF, hypoxia, nucleotide/ribonucleotide depletion, ROS...) converge on ONE protein. Normally p53 is made and
destroyed continuously (half-life 20 min) by Mdm2, whose own gene p53 induces (negative feedback). Alarms block Mdm2 -> p53 accumulates
within minutes -> transcription of p21 (arrest), repair genes, Mdm2, or pro-apoptotic genes (PUMA, Noxa, Bax, Fas, IGFBP-3) -> arrest,
senescence or apoptosis. Losing p53 blinds the cell to all these alarms at once: most mutated gene in cancer (~half of tumors), rest disable it
via ARF loss, Mdm2/MdmX amplification, viral E6/large T. Missense mutants act dominant-negative in the tetramer (15/16 tetramers poisoned).
Apoptosis = orderly suicide: Bcl-2 family balance decides mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) -> cytochrome c -> apoptosome
-> caspase 9 -> caspases 3/6/7 -> cell dismantled within an hour. Cancer cells block it in many ways (Bcl-2 overexpressed in ~half of tumors).

1. Discovery 1979 (Linzer & Levine): 53-54 kD protein co-precipitates with SV40 large T; present in uninfected embryonal carcinoma and chemically transformed cells -> cellular. Table 9.1: viruses targeting pRb (large T, E1A, E7, v-cyclins) and p53 (large T, E1B55K, E6) and apoptosis (E1B19K, v-Bcl-2, v-FLIP...).
2. First thought an oncogene (cDNA from tumor = mutant p53 cooperated with ras). Wild-type cDNA SUPPRESSES ras+myc transformation (Michalovitz 1990) -> tumor suppressor. Single point mutation difference. By 1987 p53 point mutations common; most frequently mutated gene in human cancers. Fig 9.3 (Donehower 2019, >10,000 patients, 32 types): >90% ovarian serous & uterine carcinosarcoma; near zero thyroid, uveal melanoma, pheochromocytoma (read values from figure if needed).
   p53-/- mice develop normally (not a regulator of normal proliferation!) but die of lymphomas/sarcomas; all dead by ~250 days (~5 months life span); p53+/- start dying ~250 days; wild type almost no mortality until ~500 days (Jacks 1994).
3. Li-Fraumeni (1982): families with glioblastoma, leukemia, breast, lung, pancreas carcinomas, Wilms, sarcomas; up to half of members; 2/3 some cancer by age 22. ~70% of families carry germ-line p53 mutation (17p13). Onset ages: adrenocortical ~5 y, sarcoma 16, brain 25, breast 37, lung ~50. ~900 families; mutations distributed like somatic ones in >30,000 tumors.
4. Dominant-negative: most p53 mutations are MISSENSE (unlike APC, BRCA1, ATM: frameshift/nonsense); 95.1% in DNA-binding domain. p53 is a tetramer; with 1 mutant + 1 wt allele equally expressed, only 1/16 tetramers all-wild-type (15/16 contain >=1 mutant). Knock-in point mutant (one copy) in ES cells kills almost all function; null allele in one copy leaves function almost normal. Tumors still usually lose wt allele by LOH later. Gain-of-function activities (bind p63, p73...): "both oncogene and TSG".
5. p53 half-life 20 min (cycloheximide). Rapid control by stability: cell can double p53 in 20 min by blocking degradation. Inducers (early 1990s): X-rays, UV, DNA-damaging drugs, DNA synthesis inhibitors (no mRNA change); later hypoxia, E1A, Myc, excess E2F1, DNA demethylation, nucleotide deficits, ROS, NO, acidic medium, RNA/DNA synthesis block.
   X-rays -> p53 up -> p21Cip1 mRNA up (arrest). Thymocytes after X-ray: p53+/+ 80% loss of viability in 25 h; p53+/- almost as much; p53-/- <5% (Lowe 1993).
6. Sensors: DSBs -> ATM (even a single DSB can raise p53) -> phosphorylates p53 + Chk2; ssDNA at stalled forks -> ATR -> Chk1. Phosphorylation of p53 N-terminus blocks Mdm2 binding; ATM also phosphorylates Mdm2 (inactivates). Ataxia telangiectasia = ATM deficiency.
   UV-B: p53 kills damaged keratinocytes (peeling after sunburn); p53 mutations at dipyrimidine sites in skin SCC and in sun-damaged normal skin; p53 also induces aMSH -> tanning. HPV E6 targets p53 for degradation.
7. Mdm2 (mouse double minute: amplified on DMs in sarcoma) = E3 ubiquitin ligase for p53; p53 induces Mdm2 -> negative feedback loop. Mdm2 blocks transactivation, exports p53, ubiquitylates -> proteasome. MdmX (Mdm4) partner. Mdm2 amplified in sarcomas. Akt phosphorylates Mdm2 -> nuclear -> more p53 degradation.
   Mdm2-/- embryos die early from runaway p53; Mdm2-/-;p53-/- develop normally.
   Chk2 germ-line mutant allele 1.1-1.4% of Western population, up to 5% of breast cancer cohorts. MDM2 SNP309 (T->G, homozygous ~12% of Americans): up to 8x MDM2 RNA, >3x protein; Li-Fraumeni first cancer age 22 vs 27; sarcoma age 2 vs 12; breast cancer onset 45 vs 57 in general population homozygotes.
8. ARF (p19ARF mouse / p14ARF human): alternative reading frame of p16INK4A locus (own promoter 13 kb upstream, shares exon 2). ARF sequesters Mdm2 in nucleolus -> p53 rises. ARF growth inhibition needs p53. ARF promoter has 4 E2F sites: excess E2F (pRb pathway broken) -> ARF -> p53 -> apoptosis. Chain: pRb -| E2F -> ARF -| Mdm2 -| p53 -> apoptosis. Also Ras/Raf hyperactivity -> ARF. p16INK4A/ARF (+p15INK4B) lost via ~40 kb deletion; locus inactivated in ~half of human tumors ("too many eggs in one basket").
   Myc is strongly pro-apoptotic: in lymphoid tissue Myc raises proliferation but no net cell gain (apoptosis); with p53 or ARF loss -> lymphoma (ARF+/- Emu-myc mice die much faster, lose remaining ARF allele). Rb-/- embryos die mid-gestation with E2F-driven apoptosis.
9. p53 = TF binding two 10-nt half-sites (RRRCWWGYYY x2) separated by up to 20 nt; 1546 genomic sites by ChIP. Targets: Mdm2, p21Cip1 (CDK2, CDK1 inhibition; PCNA), 14-3-3sigma (sequesters cyclin B-CDC2), DNA repair genes, Bax, PUMA, Noxa, Fas, IGFBP-3, Tsp-1 (anti-angiogenic), ferroptosis. ~150 PTMs of p53.
   Mutant p53 accumulates to high levels (can't induce Mdm2) -> immunostaining marker; SV40 large T raises p53 half-life from 20 min to 24 h.
   ENU on pregnant p53+/- x p53+/- mice: brain tumors in 70% of p53-/- pups, 3.6% of p53+/-, 0% of p53+/+ -> "guardian of the genome".
10. Senescence: irreversible arrest, flat "fried egg" cells, SA-b-gal (pH 6), SAHF heterochromatin foci, p16INK4A & p21 up, SASP (inflammatory cytokines). Endometrial fibroblasts 40-43 population doublings in culture. Needs both p53 and pRb pathways (large T delays). Oncogene-induced senescence (OIS): ras in normal fibroblasts -> senescence; in p53-null -> transformation. Nevi (moles) with BRAF mutations stay arrested for decades (p16 up, SA-b-gal). Restoring p53 for 4 days in liver tumors (ras + p53 shRNA) -> senescence and tumor clearance by innate immune cells within ~11 days (Xue 2007). SASP can also promote tumors.
11. Apoptosis in normal life: >half of initially formed neurons eliminated; webbing between digits; intestinal cells after 4-5 days; >95% of erythroblasts destroyed normally (EPO rescues); up to 90% of mammary epithelial cells die at involution; yearly cell turnover ~4x total cell number (~3x10^13).
    Morphology: blebs, pyknosis, DNA ladder, apoptotic bodies eaten; phosphatidylserine flipped outside = "eat me" (annexin). Within an hour. No inflammation (vs necrosis: swelling, lysis, inflammation; Table 9.2). Rediscovered 1972; C. elegans genetics late 1980s.
12. Bcl-2: t(14;18) in follicular B-cell lymphoma. Bcl-2 transgenic mice: no increased mortality (cells live longer, don't proliferate); Myc transgenic: almost all dead of lymphoma by 100 days; Myc + Bcl-2: all dead by ~50 days (Strasser 1990). Bcl-xL + Myc in plasma cells: tumors from ~75 days, all dead by 200 days, vs Myc alone rare tumors at ~10 months.
    Mitochondria: cytochrome c between membranes; MOMP -> release. Bcl-2 family (>=24): pore formers Bax/Bak; anti-apoptotic Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Mcl-1, Bcl-W, A1; BH3-only sensors Bid, Bim, Noxa, Bad, PUMA. Balance decides. Bcl-2-/- mice: kidney failure, immune collapse; rescued by losing ONE Bim copy. Bim -> anoikis (mammary lumen formation). Akt/Raf phosphorylate Bad -> 14-3-3 sequesters it.
    Cytochrome c + 7 Apaf-1 -> apoptosome ("wheel of death") -> procaspase 9 -> caspase 9 (>=1000x activity) -> executioner caspases 3, 6, 7 -> cleave "death substrates" (lamins, ICAD -> DNase, actin, plectin, vimentin, gelsolin); 400-1000 proteins cleaved; 12 caspase genes. Cascade amplifies. IAPs inhibit caspases; Smac/DIABLO (also from mitochondria) inhibits IAPs. Akt phosphorylates and inhibits caspase 9.
13. Extrinsic pathway: death receptors (TNF family: FAS/CD95-FasL, TNFR1-TNF-a, DR3, DR4/DR5-TRAIL) trimerize -> FADD -> DISC -> caspases 8, 10 -> executioner caspases; caspase 8 cleaves Bid -> tBid -> MOMP (links to intrinsic). CTLs kill via FasL and granzyme B. p53 induces Fas, IGFBP-3 (sequesters survival factor IGF-1/2: "death by neglect").
14. Table 9.4 anti-apoptotic alterations: p53 mutation, ARF loss, Mdm2 overexpression, Bcl-2 overexpression (~1/2 of tumors), APAF1 methylation (melanoma), BAX mutation (>half of MSI colon cancers), caspase 8 loss (N-myc neuroblastoma), FLIP, survivin, IAP-1 amplification, PTEN loss/Akt, NF-kB, IGF overexpression, TNFR1 methylation...
15. p53 loss helps at many steps: survive oncogene activation (Myc apoptosis), hypoxia, genomic instability, telomere collapse, angiogenesis (Tsp-1 down), therapy resistance (context dependent). DNA damage signaling (phospho-ATM/Chk2) already active in premalignant bladder, breast, lung, colon lesions with wild-type p53.
16. Other deaths: necrosis (tumor necrotic cores; bulk of many tumors inert necrotic tissue), autophagy (cells can consume 2/3 of own mass and recover; Beclin-1), necroptosis (RIPK1/RIPK3 -> MLKL), pyroptosis (caspase 1, gasdermin D), ferroptosis (iron, lipid peroxidation, GPX4; EMT cells sensitive; p53 promotes).
17. >100,000 p53 papers since 1979. Obesity 2nd risk factor after tobacco in US; maybe via reduced apoptosis (unproven). ~10^16 cell divisions per lifetime yet most people don't get cancer: attrition by death programs.
