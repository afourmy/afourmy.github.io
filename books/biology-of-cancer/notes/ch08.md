# Ch 8 pRb and Control of the Cell Cycle Clock (book p.275-320; raw 37159-42008)

GIST: All mitogenic and anti-mitogenic signals converge on ONE decision: pass the restriction (R) point in late G1 or not.
Before R: cell listens to the outside (needs growth factors; TGF-b can stop it). After R: autonomous program S-G2-M runs to completion.
pRb is the gatekeeper of R: hypophosphorylated pRb binds E2F transcription factors (holds gate shut); cyclin D-CDK4/6 (made in response
to mitogens) monophosphorylate; cyclin E-CDK2 hyperphosphorylates (~14 sites) -> pRb releases E2Fs -> E2Fs induce cyclin E and E2F1
themselves (positive feedback) + destroy p27 (second positive loop) -> switch-like, irreversible.
Cancer disables this gate in virtually all tumors (Rb mutation/methylation, HPV E7, cyclin D1 amplification, p16INK4A loss, CDK4 R24C,
p27 loss/mislocalization, Myc overexpression). Cancer cells don't cycle faster; they keep re-entering the cycle when normal cells would stop.

1. Cell cycle phases: G1 (12-15 h in many cultured cells; decisions), S (6-8 h; replicate ~6.4x10^9 bp diploid), G2 (3-5 h), M (~1 h; prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase, cytokinesis). G0 quiescent. Lymphocytes double in 5-6 h; early embryos 15 min.
   Checkpoints: no S if DNA damaged; slow S on damage; no M until replication complete; no anaphase until all chromosomes attached to spindle; decatenation. Cancer cells inactivate checkpoints -> faster mutation accumulation.
   Body ~10^14 cells (ch8 says "almost 10^14"; ch2 says 3x10^13 - note discrepancy).
   ES cells: pRb (and p53) control not functional; only wild-type tumorigenic cells (teratomas).
2. R point: remove serum before 80-90% of G1 -> cell returns to G0; after -> completes cycle. TGF-b only acts early/mid G1. Anchorage checkpoint in late G1 (integrins); nutrient & ROS checkpoints.
3. Cyclin-CDK: CDKs Ser/Thr kinases (~40% identity; ~430 Ser/Thr kinases in genome). Cyclin A binding raises CDK2 activity 400,000x. D-type cyclins (D1,D2,D3)+CDK4/6 (early/mid G1); E+CDK2 (R point, G1/S); A+CDK2 (S); A+CDK1 (late S/G2); B+CDK1 (M). Cyclins rise gradually, destroyed abruptly by ubiquitin ligases -> ratchet (clock only moves forward). Cyclin B oscillation in frog/sea urchin embryos gave name.
   D-cyclins are the exception: levels track extracellular mitogens (Ras->ERK->AP-1->cyclin D1 transcription); half-life ~30 min when GF removed. D cyclins = messenger from environment to clock. Cyclin D1 also binds ER, C/EBPb; cyclin D1-/- mice: underdeveloped mammary glands; ~700 genomic binding sites.
4. CDK inhibitors (CKIs): INK4 (p16INK4A, p15INK4B, p18, p19) inhibit CDK4/6 only; Cip/Kip (p21Cip1, p27Kip1, p57Kip2) inhibit CDK2 complexes but paradoxically assemble/allow D-CDK4/6 activity.
   TGF-b -> p15INK4B mRNA 30x in keratinocytes (Hannon & Beach 1994); weakly p21. DNA damage -> strong p21 (also blocks PCNA).
   Akt phosphorylates p21 (exported to cytoplasm) and p27 (kept in cytoplasm); also represses p27 transcription via FOXO and activates Skp2. High-grade breast cancer: cytoplasmic p27.
   Timing mechanism: G0 cells have lots of p27 bound to few E-CDK2. Mitogens -> D-CDK4/6 accumulate, each sequesters a p27 -> once enough, free E-CDK2 -> phosphorylates p27 (T187) -> Skp2 -> destroyed -> more free E-CDK2 (explosive). R point timing = rate of D-CDK4/6 accumulation.
   CDK activation needs CAK phosphorylation; inhibitory phosphates removed by CDC25A/B/C (CDC25B/C trigger G2/M).
5. pRb: 105 kD nuclear phosphoprotein; unphosphorylated after M (PP1 strips phosphates); monophosphorylated (any one of ~14 sites) by D-CDK4/6 in G1; hyperphosphorylated (>=12 more sites; >10x increase) by E-CDK2 at R point; stays hyper until M.
   1988 (Whyte et al.): adenovirus E1A co-precipitates pRb, p107, p130, p300. SV40 large T and HPV E7 also bind pRb: three unrelated viral proteins converge on pRb (convergent evolution; virus needs S phase for its DNA replication). They bind only hypophosphorylated pRb (the active, growth-inhibitory form). E7 also tags pRb for degradation. Pocket proteins: A+B domains.
   Stress (hypoxia, DNA damage, spindle disruption) can reverse pRb phosphorylation in S/G2 (PP1).
6. E2Fs: 8 members (E2F1-8, with DP1/DP2 partners except E2F7/8). Bind TTTCCCGC. E2F1/2/3 activators (late G1 window only); E2F4/5 repressors with p107/p130 (G0: E2F4/5 + p130 abundant); E2F6/7/8 repressors. Hypo-pRb bound to E2F on promoter blocks transactivation AND recruits HDAC (represses). Hyper-pRb releases -> histone acetylases -> transcription of ~500 genes (DHFR, thymidine kinase, DNA pol alpha, cyclin E, E2F1, Skp2).
   Positive feedback loops at R: E-CDK2 -> pRb hyper -> E2F -> cyclin E; E-CDK2 -> p27 degradation -> more E-CDK2. Cyclin A-CDK2 later phosphorylates E2F/DP and ends the window; E2F7/8 repress.
   Micro-injected E2F1 alone drives quiescent cells into S phase.
   Dominant-negative Ras blocks S entry in wild-type cells but not Rb-/- cells -> main job of Ras in G1 is to inactivate pRb. Chain: GF -> receptor -> Ras -> cyclins D1 and E -> pRb inactivated -> E2F -> S.
7. Myc: >70% of human tumors overexpress Myc/N-Myc/L-Myc. bHLH TF with Max; binds E-box CANNTG; >1000 targets; also releases paused RNA pol II (~50 bp downstream; Myc-Max at pause sites of 70% of active genes; amplifies already-active genes). Myc levels follow mitogens, Max constant. Mxd (Mad) displaces Myc in differentiating cells.
   Myc induces cyclin D2, CDK4, Cul1/Skp2/Cks1 (p27 degradation), E2F1/2/3; with Miz-1 represses p15INK4B, p21Cip1 (and p27) -> resistance to TGF-b. Myc-ER fusion + tamoxifen drives serum-starved G0 cells through G1 to S with no growth factors. Myc drives ribosome biogenesis, anabolism, angiogenesis; induces ID proteins (ID2 binds pRb, antagonizes MyoD).
   ras + myc cooperate in transformation; ras + E1A too (Myc and E1A both neutralize pRb, by different means).
8. TGF-b cytostasis: Smad3/4 + Miz-1 on CAGAC -> p15INK4B, p21; Smad3 + E2F4/5 + p107 repress myc. In 12 breast carcinomas, myc no longer repressed by TGF-b in all 12, though other TGF-b responses intact in 11. Cancer escapes cytostatic TGF-b but keeps invasive responses (ch14). pRb = brake lining TGF-b uses.
9. Proliferation vs differentiation coupled: Rb loss in retinal precursors -> defective rod differentiation; myc transgene liver carcinomas regress and redifferentiate when myc shut off; MyoD vs ID2; CML chronic phase (differentiation intact) -> blast crisis after 3-5 years (differentiation blocked). More differentiated tumors less aggressive.
10. Table 8.2 & Fig 8.26: ways pRb pathway is disrupted. Cyclin D1 elevated in up to half of breast cancers; CCND1 amplified in ~1/3 HNSCC; p16INK4A germline -> familial melanoma; p16 promoter methylation in sporadic tumors; CDK4 R24C (INK4-resistant) in some melanomas; Skp2 high -> low p27 -> poor survival (breast, oral, lymphoma); Notch induces Skp2. Table 8.3: >70-90% of tumors of each type have >=1 alteration in Rb pathway (Malumbres & Barbacid 2001).
    Cyclin E high in stage III breast cancer -> poor survival (Keyomarsi NEJM 2002); low-MW cyclin E -> multipolar spindles.
    CDK4/6 inhibitor added: breast cancer progression-free survival 14.5 -> 24.8 months (2016).
    D1/D2/D3 triple knockout embryos survive to ~E16; E1/E2 knockout to mid-gestation (placental defect) -> latent backup clock.
    pRb deals with the outside world; p53 monitors the inside (next chapter).
