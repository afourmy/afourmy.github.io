# Ch 2 The Nature of Cancer (book p.31-65; raw lines 5687-10206)

GIST: A tumor is a clone of the body's own cells that has progressively escaped the rules of tissue construction.
Most common cancers have assignable environmental/lifestyle causes (not genes). Many carcinogens act by mutating DNA
(Ames: mutagenic potency tracks carcinogenic potency over 5 orders of magnitude) -> cancer as a disease of mutant genes.
But ~40% of rodent carcinogens are NOT mutagenic -> "tumor promoters" (ch11).

1. Tumors arise from normal tissues (19th c. histology: all cells from cells, tumors made of cells, less organized architecture).
   Cancer = disease of malfunctioning cells. Benign (local) vs malignant (invades, metastasizes). Metastases cause ~90% of cancer deaths.
   Most primary tumors are benign; benign can harm via hormones (thyroid adenoma, pituitary -> acromegaly).
2. Classification by tissue of origin (tumor cells keep traits of their cell of origin -> pathologists classify >95% by histology):
   - Carcinomas (epithelia): >80% of cancer deaths in Western world. Squamous cell carcinoma (protective epithelia: skin, oral) vs adenocarcinoma (secretory: lung, colon, breast, pancreas, stomach, prostate...).
   - Epithelium sits on a basement membrane (specialized ECM) separating it from stroma (connective tissue: fibroblasts, adipocytes, collagen).
   - Sarcomas (mesenchymal: fibroblasts, adipocytes, osteoblasts, myocytes): ~1% of tumors in clinic.
   - Hematopoietic: leukemias (circulate), lymphomas (solid in lymph nodes): ~7% of cancer mortality (US).
   - Neuroectodermal (gliomas, neuroblastoma, medulloblastoma, retinoblastoma): 1.3% of diagnosed, ~2.5% of deaths.
   - Exceptions: melanoma (neural crest), small-cell lung carcinoma (neurosecretory traits), transdifferentiation, EMT (reversible, gene expression not mutation), teratomas (~10,000/yr worldwide; genetically wild type; 3 germ layers, teeth, hair).
   - Anaplastic 2-5%: dedifferentiated; cancer of unknown primary (CUP); molecular markers now identify ~90% of CUPs.
3. Progression (suggested, not proven): normal -> hyperplasia (too many cells, normal look) / metaplasia (wrong normal cell type; Barrett's esophagus: 30x risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma, ~0.5%/year progress) -> dysplasia (cytologically abnormal: nuclear size/shape, N:C ratio, mitoses) -> adenoma/polyp/papilloma (macroscopic, dysplastic, respects basement membrane, benign) -> invasive carcinoma (breaches basement membrane into stroma) -> metastasis. DCIS = ductal carcinoma in situ, fills duct, necrotic center (no blood supply).
4. Monoclonality (one ancestral cell):
   - rare chromosomal translocation present in ALL tumor cells.
   - X-inactivation + G6PD (Supp Sidebar 2.5): >30% of African American women heterozygous for G6PD alleles on X; each cell expresses one allele (random X inactivation early embryo, inherited by descendants -> tissue is mosaic of patches). 1965: every leiomyoma expressed only ONE variant. Fialkow 1974.
   - Multiple myeloma: all cells make identical antibody (M-spike) vs polyclonal normal plasma.
   - Caveat: 10 founder clones with slightly different doubling times -> fastest outgrows all -> looks monoclonal. Monoclonality of a large tumor does not prove monoclonal origin.
   - Later diversification: subclones; phylogenetic tree; "truncal mutations" shared by all (e.g. VHL in kidney carcinoma; Gerlinger NEJM 2012: SETD2 mutated independently in 3 subclones).
5. Numbers: ~3x10^13 cells in adult; ~10^16 cells formed in a lifetime (~10^7 death/replacement events per second).
   Thought experiment: if lifestyle changes halved cancer deaths, cancer ~10% of mortality -> only 1 in 10^17 cell divisions (divisions of 10 lifetimes) leads to clinically detectable cancer -> body has very effective defenses.
6. Epidemiology: incidence varies hugely by country (Table 2.5, relative risk high/low): melanoma Queensland vs Japan 155; lip Newfoundland vs Japan 151; nasopharynx Hong Kong vs UK 100; prostate US African American vs China 70; liver Shanghai vs Nova Scotia 49; penis 42; cervix 28; stomach Japan vs Kuwait 22; lung 19; pancreas 11; ovary 8. Death rates: lung male E.Europe vs W.Africa 33; esophagus 16; colon male 15; breast 6 (N.Europe vs China).
   - Diagnostic bias (Sidebar 2.1): mammography finds indolent growths; maybe 2/3 of US "breast cancers" indolent; age-adjusted mortality more reliable. US cancer mortality down ~35% over a generation.
   - Migrants: Japanese stomach cancer 6-8x Americans; offspring in US (Hawaii) converge within a generation (Fig 2.18, Peto 2001) -> environment not heredity.
   - Seventh-day Adventists: cancer death 40% lower (men), 24% lower (women).
   - "as many as 85% of US breast cancers avoidable in theory" (China 1/6) with big caveat.
7. History: John Hill 1761 snuff -> nasal cancer; Percivall Pott 1775 chimney sweeps scrotal cancer; Danish sweepers bathe -> less. 1839 nuns breast cancer 6x. X-ray tube users. Doll & Hill 1950: heavy smokers >20x lung cancer risk.
   Table 2.6 (Doll & Hill BMJ 1950) relative risk by cigarettes/day: nonsmoker 1; 1-5: 8; 5-15: 12; 15-25: 14; >25: 27.
   Table 2.7 US causes % of cases: occupational 1-2; tobacco 34; diet (low veg, nitrates, salt) 5; diet (high fat, low fiber) 37; tobacco+alcohol 2. Specific agents: vinyl chloride->liver angiosarcoma, benzene->leukemia, asbestos->mesothelioma, radium->osteosarcoma, DES->vaginal carcinoma...
   Fig 2.19 (Islami 2018): US 2014 ~587,000 cancer deaths; ~45% attributable to modifiable factors (265,150 deaths); smoking+obesity+alcohol = 39.3%. (Other bar labels missing in OCR; would need figure image.)
8. Chemical carcinogenesis: Yamagiwa 1915 coal tar on rabbit ears (carcinoma after 660 days of painting). Nobel 1926 to Fibiger (worms, discredited). By 1940 PAHs purified: 3-methylcholanthrene, benzo[a]pyrene (in cigarette smoke), dibenz[a,h]anthracene.
9. Carcinogens as mutagens: Muller 1927 X-rays mutate Drosophila; alkylating agents (mustard gas) mutagenic & carcinogenic; Boveri 1914 chromosomes aberrant; Philadelphia chromosome 1960 in CML (t(9;22), tiny 22q-).
   Ames 1975: his- Salmonella; mutagen reverts -> colony on histidine-free plate; + rat liver homogenate to convert procarcinogen -> ultimate carcinogen (liver detox enzymes do it). Mutagenic vs carcinogenic potency correlate on log-log over 5 orders of magnitude (Fig 2.21B, Meselson & Russell 1977). Carcinogen potency varies up to 10^6-fold per weight; 1 ug aflatoxin = 10,000x weight of benzidine. By 1976, 300 compounds.
   Caveats: ~40% of rodent carcinogens non-mutagenic in Ames (by 1990s) -> tumor promoters (inflammation). Ames sensitivity ~54%, specificity ~70%. Tobacco weak in rodents; asbestos undetectable by lab tests; saccharin carcinogen in male rats but not humans. Natural dietary mutagens (Table S2.1).
