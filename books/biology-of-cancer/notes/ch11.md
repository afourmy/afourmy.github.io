# Ch 11 Multi-Step Tumorigenesis (book p.415-477; raw 54352-61158, supp sidebars to 63040)

GIST: A human tumor forms by a succession of rare, independent heritable changes (4-6 driver mutations plus epigenetic ones),
each triggering a clonal expansion: Darwinian evolution among cells. Each step breaches one of the barriers that evolution built
into large, long-lived animals (Peto's paradox: 10^16 vs 10^11 divisions human vs mouse, yet equal cancer risk).
Evidence: (1) incidence rises as age^5-6 (Armitage-Doll/Nordling: slope k-1 on log-log => k rate-limiting events);
(2) histology normal -> hyperplasia -> dysplasia -> adenoma -> carcinoma with matching genetic changes (Vogelstein 1989:
APC(5q) -> K-ras -> 18q (Smad4) -> p53 (17p)); (3) reconstruction: one oncogene never transforms a truly normal cell;
ras+myc transform rodent cells; human cells need 5 pathways (Ras, pRb, p53, telomerase, PP2A).
Second big idea: most carcinogens are NOT mutagens but tumor PROMOTERS: they speed the clonal expansions (initiation /
promotion / progression, mouse skin DMBA + TPA). Promotion is reversible, mitogenic (estrogen) or inflammatory
(TNF-a -> NF-kB -> COX-2 -> PGE2; NSAIDs cut risk). Late-stage genetic instability makes tumors branch into diverse
subclones (heterogeneity -> relapse); cancer stem cells add a phenotypic hierarchy. Warburg effect: aerobic glycolysis.

11.1 Age: US colon cancer death risk up to 1000x higher at 70 than 10. Lung cancer rose ~30 years after WWII smoking;
  asbestos mesothelioma lag ~40 years. Supp 11.1: incidence ~ a^4 to a^7 (a = age); epithelial cancers as a whole ~a^5-a^6;
  "if probability ~ a^n then n+1 independent events"; each event on average once every 10-15 years; whole succession 40-60 years.
  Nordling 1953 log-log plots of male cancer death rates 1939-47 in 4 countries: slope 5 => 6 rate-limiting events; similar slopes in
  all countries. Real number of steps likely higher (fast steps don't register). Autopsies: 60-70% of people carry undiagnosed tumors
  by life's end. Duration of carcinogen exposure (not age) sets onset (mesothelioma in insulation workers; BP-painted mice; J. Peto 2001).
  Pediatric cancers and testicular cancer don't follow the power law. Supp 11.2: curing all cancer (Netherlands 1990) adds only
  3.83 years life expectancy (men), 3.38 (women).
11.2 Histopathology: gut epithelium one cell thick; 20-50 million cells die/replaced per minute in duodenum (1/10 as many in colon).
  Series: normal crypt -> aberrant crypt -> tubular/villous adenoma (polyp, benign: basement membrane intact) -> invasive carcinoma
  -> liver metastasis. Evidence adenoma->carcinoma: (a) carcinoma seen growing out of adenoma; (b) polypectomy after colonoscopy cuts
  subsequent colorectal cancer >80% (conservative 60%) vs expected (Winawer 1993) => >=60% of CRC arise from detectable adenomas;
  (c) longitudinal (3 lung CIS lesions, same rare p53 mutation; one became SCC by 19 months). FAP: conversion ~2.5 per 1000 polyps per year.
  Parallel sequences in other organs (CIN cervix, DCIS breast, PIN prostate). Not all early lesions progress -> overtreatment.
11.3 Genetics: Sidebar 11.3 mutation rate ~10^-9 per bp per cell cycle; ~10^16 divisions per lifetime; barriers are hardwired in DNA
  sequence because DNA is the most stable storage -> mutations (4-6) required; epigenetic changes alone rarely suffice.
  Vogelstein 1989 (Johns Hopkins): small adenomas LOH 5q (APC); ~half of larger adenomas +K-ras; larger adenomas 18q LOH; ~half of
  carcinomas 17p LOH (p53). TSGs outnumber oncogenes. ras and PI3K mutations rarely co-occur (redundant). Hypomethylation early
  -> chromosomal instability. Sidebar 11.4: most efficient order APC -> K-ras -> Smad4 -> p53; pathways, not genes, matter:
  beta-catenin can replace APC; B-Raf or PI3K can replace Ras; TGF-b receptor methylation replaces Smad4. p53 mutation late
  (carcinoma, not adenoma). Li-Fraumeni patients rarely get CRC (APC must start it).
  Hallmarks (Hanahan & Weinberg): 1 autonomous proliferation, 2 resistance to anti-growth signals, 3 resistance to death,
  4 angiogenesis, 5 immortality, 6 invasion/metastasis; added 7 immune evasion, 8 deregulated energy metabolism; enabling:
  genome instability (mutability) and inflammation. No 1-to-1 map genes -> hallmarks (large T hits p53 -> 3 traits, pRb -> 2; Ras
  -> apoptosis, mitogen independence, angiogenesis, invasion; hTERT -> immortality only).
  ~95% of tumors retain differentiation traits of cell of origin (allows pathology classification; luminal vs basal breast).
11.4 Darwinian clonal succession (Nowell mid-1970s): clone grows to ~10^6 cells, then next mutation (rate ~10^-6 per cell generation)
  becomes likely -> new clone takes over. New mutation must be complementary (not redundant). TSG inactivation = 2 steps ->
  number of alterations up to ~2x number of loci. Intervals: a decade or more between CRC steps; polyp -> invasive cancer up to 17 years;
  whole process may stretch over a century; diet may compress intervals to <=5 years -> 30-40 years total. Colon cancer varies 20-fold
  internationally (migrants converge). HNPCC (DNA repair defect) accelerates. Aged microenvironment more permissive.
11.5 FAP: every colon cell already APC+/- -> first rate-limiting step skipped -> hundreds-thousands of polyps. Field cancerization:
  large clone of histologically normal mutant cells (p53-mutant patches up to 3000 cells in sun-exposed skin; fields many cm wide in
  bladder, oral mucosa, esophagus, lung) -> several "independent" tumors that share mutations. Clonal hematopoiesis: blood equivalent,
  raises leukemia risk.
11.6 Late-stage genetic instability outruns selection -> branching evolution, heterogeneity; purifying selection early vs diversification
  late. Renal cell carcinoma (Gerlinger 2012): VHL truncal; SETD2, KDM5C mutated independently in different regions (convergent);
  branches spawning metastases. Pancreatic carcinoma subclones each >=10^8 cells, each seeding own metastases. Passengers >> drivers.
  FISH: chromosome 17 copy number varies cell-to-cell. Epigenetic phenotypic diversity too (EGFR/VEGFR patchy).
11.7 Cancer stem cells: AML (Bonnet & Dick): minority <1%; 5000 minority cells seed leukemia in mice, 500,000 majority cells don't.
  Breast (Al-Hajj 2003): CD44hi CD24lo ESA+ ~2% (12% before ESA); 200 cells form tumor, 20,000 other cells don't; genetically identical.
  Hierarchy: stem -> transit-amplifying/progenitor -> post-mitotic differentiated; plasticity (dedifferentiation). Niche: Paneth cells
  give Wnt to Lgr5+ crypt stem cells; lung adenocarcinoma Porcupine+ (Wnt-making) next to Lgr5+ (Wnt-responding). CSCs more resistant
  to chemo/radio/immunotherapy -> relapse; metastasis seeders must be CSCs. EMT spectrum.
11.8 One oncogene is not enough: NIH 3T3 transformed by ras alone, but NIH 3T3 is an immortalized line, not normal. Calculation:
  ~3x10^11 divisions per day in body; several thousand new ras point mutants per day; millions of ras-mutant cells in body - yet few tumors.
  Primary rat/hamster embryo cells not transformed by ras alone. ras in mouse colon -> hyperplasia only; DMBA ras mutants in skin
  detectable in a week but skin normal a year. ACFs with APC + K-ras still not adenomas. Germ-line Kit (GIST) and H-ras carriers get
  tumors decades later. Identical-twin leukemias share in-utero clone but diagnosed years apart (ages 1-13).
  Land, Parada & Weinberg 1983: ras + myc (or E1A) transform rat embryo fibroblasts; neither alone. ras: anchorage independence, loss
  of contact inhibition, refractile; myc: immortalization, less growth-factor dependence. Table 11.1 pairs (ras+SV40 LT, Notch+E1A,
  middle T + large T, raf+myc, erbB+erbA, pim1+myc, abl+myc, bcl-2+myc). Table 11.2 mechanisms (ras gives proliferation+senescence,
  E1A prevents senescence; myc gives proliferation+apoptosis, bcl-2 blocks apoptosis; erbB proliferation, erbA blocks differentiation).
  Sinn 1987 MMTV-ras, MMTV-myc, double transgenic mice: double much faster (Fig 11.26, T50 values - read from figure if needed);
  even double needs further stochastic event (often p53 loss).
11.9 Human cells: rodent ~80 My divergence; mouse cells immortalize spontaneously, human cells never; 15 years of failed transfection.
  Hahn et al. 1999: hTERT + SV40 early region (LT: pRb + p53; small t: PP2A) + H-ras => tumorigenic human cells. Five pathways:
  Ras, pRb, p53, telomeres (hTERT), PP2A (B56g subunit). PP2A: 2 A x 18 B x 2 C subunits, ~30 heterotrimers; small t displaces B;
  nonphosphorylatable Myc can replace small t. Glioblastoma survey: 74% alter pRb+p53+Ras-MAPK pathways; nearly all hTERT up.
  PCAWG: 2658 genomes, 38 cancer types, average 4-5 driver mutations per genome. Colon organoids (2015): APC, p53, K-ras, Smad4 in
  Lgr5+ stem cells -> invasive CRC; each mutation replaced one medium factor (Wnt, R-spondin, EGF, Noggin). ~half a dozen rate-limiting events.
11.10 Peto's paradox: mouse ~10^11 mitoses per lifetime, human ~10^16 (mass x lifespan); bowhead whale 100 tons, 200 years -> naive risk
  10,000x ours; whales show accelerated evolution of TSGs (2021). >=10 mammalian orders from ~25 g ancestor independently evolved large
  size in last 100 My -> each evolved its own defenses. # oncogenes needed correlates with body size/lifespan (Fig 11.28B); rodent TERT
  inversely with body mass. Pediatric tumors: very few mutations (rhabdoid: ~1 recurring mutation; retinoblastoma); ES cells tumorigenic
  (teratomas) with 0 mutations; adult human cells ~5.
11.11 Non-mutagenic carcinogens. Beyond tobacco smoke, UV, aflatoxin B1 and ethanol (acetaldehyde), few human cancers link to specific
  mutagens. Sidebar 11.10: 20 suspected human carcinogens tested in mice, all caused tumors; sequencing of 188 mouse tumors: only 3 raised
  mutation burden, 17 did not. Mouse skin (1940s): DMBA once (initiator) + TPA weekly (promoter, croton seed, activates PKC-a via DAG
  mimicry): papillomas after 4-8 weeks (strain). DMBA alone: nothing; TPA alone: nothing; DMBA then 1 year rest then TPA: papillomas
  (memory = mutation); TPA on nearby unexposed patch: nothing; stop TPA early: papillomas regress (reversible = non-genetic); continue
  TPA: some promoter-independent; second DMBA on papilloma -> carcinoma (progression). DMBA gives H-ras (codon 61) mutation;
  progression adds p53 mutation. Promoters: expand initiated clones (so next rare mutation becomes likely), or drive proliferation
  -> replication errors. Random mutations accumulate at similar rates in tissues with very different cancer rates -> promotion rate-limiting.
11.12 Mitogens as promoters: steroid hormones. Breast cancer risk falls 20% per year of delayed menarche; twins: earlier menarche twin 5.4x
  more likely to be first diagnosed; menopause before 45 -> half risk vs >=55; Dutch Hodgkin survivors with chemo-menopause before 36:
  90% lower breast cancer risk; postmenopausal patients 15% higher circulating estrogen. GEMM limits (Sidebar 11.12).
11.13 Inflammation: TPA is an irritant (neutrophils); anti-inflammatory strategies block TPA promotion; wounding as effective as TPA.
  Colonic adenoma cells non-tumorigenic in nude mice unless implanted with plastic fragment (inflammation) -> then tumorigenic even after
  retransplantation. Table 11.3: bladder/schistosomiasis, stomach/H. pylori, liver/HBV-HCV, lung/silica+smoke, mesothelioma/asbestos,
  ovary/endometriosis, colon/IBD, esophagus/acid reflux, thyroid/thyroiditis, prostate/prostatitis, gallbladder/gallstones.
  HBV ~100x liver cancer risk over decades; HCV similar (inflammation, not viral specifics). Shanghai: HBV ~7x, aflatoxin ~3x, both ~60x
  (synergy initiator x promoter). IBD smokers ~2x CRC (2021).
11.14 NSAIDs (multi-year, usually 5 years daily): pancreatic -46%, colorectal ~-30%, breast -25%, non-cardia stomach ~-40% in H. pylori
  carriers only. But NSAID GI bleeding: 16,550 US deaths in 1998. TNF-a knockout mice: 5-10% as many skin tumors (DMBA+TPA 15 weeks).
  Pathway: TPA -> PKC-a -> IKK -> IkB -> NF-kB -> TNF-a -> inflammation; NF-kB -> Bcl-xL, IAPs (survival), Myc, cyclin D1 (proliferation),
  COX-2 -> PGE2. Promotion ~ wound-healing program. Complete carcinogens (BP): initiator + promoter. No Ames-like test for promoters;
  threshold (non-linear) dose responses -> mouse high-dose results don't translate.
11.15 Warburg (1924): aerobic glycolysis: glycolysis gives 2 ATP/glucose vs 33-36 with TCA+OXPHOS; ~80% of cancers mostly glycolytic;
  mitochondria still work (Warburg wrong on that). GLUT1 up (myc, ras, src, HIF-1a) -> FDG-PET imaging. Rationale: glycolytic intermediates
  diverted to biosynthesis (nucleotides, lipids); normal cells use >30% of glucose for ATP, cancer ~1%. PK-M2 (embryonic) slow isoform,
  inhibited by phosphotyrosine proteins -> backup of intermediates, lactate; PK-M2, GLUT1, LDH-A needed for tumor growth. Glutamine
  addiction. Rapidly dividing normal cells do the same (mitogens raise GLUT1).
11.16 COSMIC 2020: >1% of genes (>200) implicated; >90% somatic, 20% germ-line. Worldwide: H. pylori 9% of cancer deaths, liver (HBV/HCV)
  6%, cervix (HPV 16, 18, 45) 5%. 450 million chronic HBV, 200 million HCV. Oncogene addiction: switching off transgenic myc/ras often
  collapses tumors; sometimes (neu, myc, wnt) relapse without them.
