# Ch 6 Cytoplasmic Signaling Circuitry Programs Many of the Traits of Cancer (book p.171-231; raw 24731-31660)

GIST: Cancer cells use the SAME circuitry as normal cells, with a few components stuck. "Cancer is a disease of aberrant signal processing."
The key design trick: a phosphorylated receptor tail is a set of docking addresses. SH2 domains (~100 aa) recognize a phosphotyrosine
+ 3-6 flanking residues -> signaling proteins are RELOCATED to the membrane, next to their substrates (Ras, PIP2). Relocalization, not
direct activation, is the main mechanism. Each RTK displays its own set of phosphotyrosines -> own set of partners -> own response.
Main pathway: GF -> RTK -> Grb2 (SH2 + 2 SH3, pure adaptor) -> Sos (GEF) -> Ras -> Raf -> MEK -> ERK (MAPK cascade) -> nucleus (Fos/Jun, Myc, cyclin D1).
Ras has 3 effector arms: Raf (proliferation), PI3K -> PIP3 -> Akt (survival, growth, metabolism; PTEN reverses), Ral-GEF (trafficking, motility).
Signals are always shut off by negative feedback (Ras-GAP, Sprouty, phosphatases, endocytosis); cancer breaks both accelerators and brakes.

1. Serum-starved fibroblasts (G0) + serum -> DNA synthesis after 9-12 h. Within 1 h >100 immediate early genes (IEGs) induced even with cycloheximide (no new protein) -> signaling machinery pre-exists; activation by structure/location changes. 229 IEGs of 14,824 genes surveyed (Selvaraj 2004). IEGs: fos, junB, egr-1, myc... Myc half-life 25 min; myc mRNA rises and falls with mitogen -> Myc level = sensor of mitogens. Delayed early genes need protein synthesis.
   Myc activated by LEVEL; Ras/Src by STRUCTURE (mirrors oncogene activation modes).
   Fos represses its own gene (negative feedback) -> short-term response differs from continuous state.
   ~30% of cell volume is protein; >400 Ser/Thr kinases.
2. Genetics: Drosophila eye, Sevenless (FGF-R homolog; 7th photoreceptor missing) -> Son of sevenless (Sos) = Ras GEF. Adaptors Shc, Grb2, Crk (v-Crk oncoprotein). Pathway >600 My old; components interchangeable fly/human.
3. Src domains SH1 (kinase), SH2 (binds pY + flanking), SH3 (binds proline-rich PxxP). ~120 SH2 domains in 110 human proteins; ~300 SH3 domains. SH3 >1 By old (28 in yeast); SH2 ~900 My (with tyrosine phosphorylation, first metazoa).
   PDGF-R tail pYs attract Src, PI3K, Ras-GAP, SHP-2, PLC-g (Fig 6.5). EGF-R attracts a different set. Same kinase, different pY sets -> specificity.
   Src self-inhibition: SH2 binds its own pY527; v-Src lacks Y527 tail -> constitutive. SHP-1 phosphatase (negative feedback): Finnish family EPO-R lacking SHP-1 site -> more red cells -> Olympic cross-country ski medals.
   Modular domains shuffled by evolution (Table 6.2: SH2, PTB, SH3, 14-3-3, Bromo, PH).
4. Receptor -> Grb2 -> Sos -> Ras (or Receptor -> Shc -> Grb2 -> Sos -> Ras). Sos brought to membrane next to Ras.
5. Raf-MEK-ERK (MAPKKK-MAPKK-MAPK): Ras-GTP binds Raf 1000x more tightly, brings it to membrane; scaffolds (KSR) hold kinases together (speed). Ser/Thr phosphorylation ACTIVATES (conformation) vs pY = docking. ERK -> nucleus: Ets, Elk-1, SAP-1; Fos+Jun = AP-1. 2507 ERK substrates (2017). Oncogenic Raf alone recapitulates most Ras transformation.
   B-Raf mutant in ~50% of melanomas (~500x kinase activity; V600E activation loop), N-Ras in 1/3 of the rest; ras and BRAF mutations mutually exclusive (redundant). B-Raf mutant ~1/50 transforming power of Ras G12V. B-Raf mutant in ~8% of tumor lines.
6. PI3K: phosphorylates PIP2 -> PIP3 (lipid, 3' OH). PIP3 docks PH-domain proteins (Akt, Rho-GEFs). Akt (PKB): inhibits Bad, caspase-9, FOXO; activates Mdm2 (destroys p53), IKK; inhibits GSK-3b (so b-catenin, cyclin D1, Myc stable); inhibits Tsc2 -> mTOR -> protein synthesis (cell growth); GLUT4. Constitutive Akt in pancreatic b cells -> cells >2x cross-section (~4x volume).
   PTEN removes 3' phosphate (PIP3 -> PIP2). PTEN lost in 30-40% of all cancers (mutation, methylation, miRNA). PIK3CA (p110a) 2nd most mutated oncogene after K-RAS; ~1/3 colon carcinomas; ~10% of all cancers (2020), ~1/3 breast & endometrial. PI3K activation and PTEN loss are mutually exclusive (same output). p110a knockout abolishes K-ras pancreatic cancer in mice.
   Table 6.4 PI3K pathway alterations by cancer type.
7. Ral-GEF -> RalA/B -> Sec5, Exo84 (exocyst, anchorage independence), RalBP1 (Cdc42, Rac; motility). 151 small G proteins (Ras superfamily: Rho, Rac, Cdc42 (filopodia), Ran...). Ras vs Rho diverged >=1.5 Bya.
   Oversimplified: Raf -> proliferation; PI3K -> survival & metabolism (aerobic glycolysis); Ral-GEF -> trafficking & adhesion. Hierarchy: mutating the top (Ras) confers more traits than mutating any single downstream branch; tumors often mutate two branches (Raf + PI3K) instead.
8. JAK-STAT: cytokine receptors (IFN, EPO, TPO), JAKs phosphorylate receptor tails, STATs dock via SH2, get phosphorylated, dimerize via reciprocal SH2-pY, go to nucleus. 7 STATs. Targets: myc, cyclins D2/D3, Bcl-XL. Engineered disulfide-linked STAT3 = oncogene. STAT3 constitutively active in many melanomas (via Src), majority of breast cancers, almost all head-and-neck cancers. STAT1 can be tumor suppressive.
9. Integrins -> FAK (clustered in focal adhesions) -> Src, Shc, PI3K, PLC-g, Grb2-Sos -> Ras. ~180 proteins in focal adhesions. Integrins and RTKs converge on same pathways -> oncogenic Ras fakes the "anchored" signal -> anchorage independence. Anoikis carves lumens of mammary acini; Bcl-xL or EGF-R overexpression -> luminal filling (like DCIS).
10. Wnt canonical: without Wnt, destruction complex (Apc, axin, Wtx, GSK-3b) phosphorylates b-catenin -> ubiquitylated -> destroyed (half-life <20 min). With Wnt: half-life 1-2 h, b-catenin to nucleus with Tcf/Lef -> cyclin D1, Myc; stemness in intestinal crypts. b-catenin also bound to E-cadherin at adherens junctions. Wnts up 4-10x in breast cancers; nuclear b-catenin ~20% advanced prostate; b-catenin mutants (unphosphorylatable) 5-7% prostate, liver, colon, endometrium, ovary, melanoma; Apc defective in virtually all colon carcinomas. Non-canonical: Ror1/2 pseudokinase RTKs, Wnt-PCP, Wnt-Ca2+ -> motility, invasion.
11. GPCRs ~830 (half olfactory), >35% of approved drugs target GPCRs, ~100 orphans. 21 Ga, 6 Gb, 12 Gg. Table 6.5: Gas mutant thyroid/pituitary; Gaq/Ga11 uveal melanoma; TSH-R mutant thyroid adenomas; >=10 of 17 Ga genes act as oncogenes. SCLC secretes bombesin/GRP, bradykinin, CCK, gastrin, neurotensin, vasopressin + their receptors (multiple autocrine loops); anti-GRP antibody: 1 complete + 4 partial responses of 12 patients. KSHV encodes a constitutive GPCR -> VEGF autocrine.
12. Dual-address pathways (cytoplasm -> nucleus): NF-kB (IkB destroyed after IKK phosphorylation; up to 500 targets; anti-apoptotic Bcl-2, IAPs; myc, cyclin D1; REL amplified in ~1/4 diffuse large B-cell lymphomas; IKKe overexpressed ~30% breast); Notch (fires once per ligand; ~60% T-ALL have activating NOTCH1 deletions; tumor suppressor in some carcinomas); Hedgehog (PTCH germline -> Gorlin syndrome: basal cell carcinomas, medulloblastoma; ~50% sporadic BCCs PTCH1 or SMO mutant; BCC most common cancer in West; Gli1 mRNA 40x in BCC; paracrine Hh to stroma in GI carcinomas); TGF-b/Smad (Smad4 lost in PanIN-3; Smads bind a 4-nt sequence, partner >130 TFs); STAT, b-catenin; Hippo/YAP-TAZ (integrates density, polarity, ECM stiffness, metabolism; YAP nuclear at low density, cytoplasmic at high density -> contact inhibition; NF2/Merlin loss keeps YAP nuclear).
13. Feedback: negative (Ras-GAP recruited to active receptor; Sprouty blocks Grb2-Sos and Raf; Cbl ubiquitylates receptor; SHP-2 dephosphorylates; endocytosis -> endosome/lysosome; Hh induces its own inhibitor Patched). Positive (NF1 degraded after PKC phosphorylation, speeding Ras activation). Aggressive breast cancers without RAS mutations lose Ras-GAPs.
    Vemurafenib (B-Raf inhibitor) works in melanoma but not in B-Raf-mutant colorectal cancer: ERK normally suppresses EGF-R (negative feedback); inhibiting B-Raf releases EGF-R -> signals via PI3K-Akt. Melanoma lacks EGF-R.
14. Three regulatory modes: (1) change activity (GTP binding, dimerization, phosphorylation, cleavage); (2) change concentration (b-catenin, PIP3); (3) change location (SH2 recruitment, nuclear translocation). Speed: kinase cascades (<1 min) > dual-address TFs > changing concentrations via transcription/translation.
15. Numbers: 518 kinase genes (>1000 kinase proteins via splicing), 90 tyrosine kinases, 58 RTKs; >100 PTPs; ~200 million possible binary interactions, ~64,000 validated (2016); >50,000 phosphopeptides; histone H4 98,304 theoretical proteoforms.
    Why tissue specificity? 90% pancreatic carcinomas mutant K-ras vs ~10% breast; EGFR/HER2 amplification >1/3 breast; ~50% colon K-ras.
    Hormone receptors (ER, AR) in breast/prostate: ER in majority of breast cancers, AR in almost all prostate cancers; mechanism of mitogenesis still unclear.
