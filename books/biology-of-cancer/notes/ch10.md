# Ch 10 Eternal Life: Cell Immortalization and Tumorigenesis (book p.375-413; raw 49241-53560)

GIST: Normal human cell lineages have a limited number of divisions; a tumor needs far more. Two barriers: senescence (stress-induced,
viable arrest; needs pRb & p53 -> bypassed by SV40 large T) and crisis (telomere collapse -> chromosome fusions -> death).
Telomeres = 5-15 kb of TTAGGG repeats + 3' G-overhang tucked into a t-loop; they lose 50-100 bp per division (end-replication
problem + exonucleases) = a cell-autonomous generation counter. Crisis at ~3 kb (TRF). Escape: telomerase (hTERT reverse transcriptase
+ hTR RNA template) de-repressed in 85-90% of tumors (e.g., hTERT promoter mutations -228/-250), ALT recombination in 10-15%.
Twist: if p53 is lost first, collapsing telomeres don't kill but drive breakage-fusion-bridge cycles -> aneuploidy, then telomerase
freezes the scrambled karyotype -> explains aneuploid carcinomas.

1. C. elegans: pedigree of all 959 somatic cells known. Human ~10^14 cells (almost), ~10^16 divisions per lifetime.
   Hayflick (mid-1960s): human fibroblasts ~60 doublings then replicative senescence (viable up to a year). Newborn cells double more than adult cells' (or time-dependent damage). Maybe partly culture artifact (Supp 10.3).
2. Senescence: p16INK4A and p21 rise; forced p16 alone mimics senescence; keratinocytes escaping early senescence lose p16 or overexpress CDK4. SV40 large T (binds pRb and p53) bypasses; mutants unable to bind either fail (HEK cells senesce after 10-12 PDs; Hahn 2002). Then crisis 10-20 generations later anyway.
   ES cells immortal. HeLa (1951, Henrietta Lacks, cervical adenocarcinoma) divides ~once a day since. Canine transmissible venereal tumor: one progenitor >6000 years ago, spread by mating.
3. Arithmetic: 1 cm^3 ~ 10^9 cells; lethal tumor ~10^3 cm^3 = 10^12 cells ~ 2^40 -> 40 doublings if pure exponential. Normal cells allow 50-60 doublings -> 60 would give 10^15-10^18 cells ~ 10^9 cm^3 ~ 10^6 kg: "something is drastically wrong". Resolution: heavy attrition each generation (apoptosis, senescence, lack of GFs/oxygen); 10^3 cells may need 20-30+ generations. Sidebar 10.2: colorectal tumors show many hundreds of generations (from mutation counts); without attrition 2^500 ~ 10^150 cells ~ 10^123 kg... (book: ~2^500 = ~10^150 cells, ~10^12 kg? OCR unclear: "~25qq=~1Q15q cells ... -1Q12 kilograms =~1Q billion times the weight of a (large) human body" -> check). If 100 generations are needed, a 50-60 allotment aborts most incipient tumors.
   Ras dose: high-level (viral vector) ras -> senescence; single-copy endogenous K-ras -> hyperplasia ("flies under the radar"); later ras amplification requires p53 loss. "The dose makes the poison."
   A generation counter can't be a diluted molecule (no molecule spans 2^50-2^60 concentration range).
4. Crisis: widespread apoptosis, karyotypic disarray; large T doesn't prevent. Linear DNA ends fuse (transfected DNA fuses). McClintock 1941 (maize): chromosomes without telomeres fuse; Nobel 1983 (transposons). TRF2 loss -> all chromosomes fuse into one.
   Telomeric DNA: TTAGGG repeated thousands of times, 5-15 kbp in humans (usually 5-10 kbp), 3' G-rich overhang 100-several hundred nt, t-loop lariat (Griffith 1999). Subtelomeric imperfect repeats 2-3 kb.
   Shortening 50-100 bp per generation (EBV lymphocytes; Counter 1994); crisis when TRFs < ~3 kbp; function lost when pure TTAGGG region ~12 repeats. Immortal escapees maintain slightly longer telomeres.
   End-replication problem: lagging strand needs RNA primer; last primer removed; ~10 nt + positioning -> 3' end of template unreplicated; plus exonucleases.
   Breakage-fusion-bridge (BFB): eroded sister chromatid ends fuse (same length since just replicated, held by centromere) -> dicentric -> anaphase bridge -> random break -> new unprotected ends -> fuse again... karyotypic chaos ("genetic catastrophe", apoptosis even without p53).
5. Telomerase: 85-90% of human tumors positive (TRAP assay); normal cells very low (exceptions: testis germ cells, activated lymphocytes, stem cells some). Expressed morula-blastocyst then lost. hTERT (catalytic RT) + hTR (451-nt RNA with template); adds TTAGGG hexamers to G-overhang. Only hTERT missing in normal cells. Escape from crisis ~1 in 10^7 cells; immortal variants express hTERT mRNA. hTERT promoter mutations (-228, -250; mutually exclusive) create ETS/GABPa sites: 83% primary glioblastomas, 66% bladder carcinomas, 71% melanomas. Myc drives hTERT; also HPV E6, ER81 (Ras-MAPK); repressed by Menin, p53, Mxd, WT1, Sp1(TGF-b).
   Ectopic hTERT in HEK cells (would enter crisis at ~40 days / ~20 PDs) -> telomeres lengthen, indefinite growth (Counter 1998).
   Rodents (15 species, 20 g-55 kg): telomerase activity inversely related to body mass (Seluanov 2007). Mice ~10^11 divisions/lifetime vs humans ~10^16 (100,000x).
   Sidebar 10.4 puzzle: hTERT also prevents senescence (stress may degrade G-overhang -> DNA damage signal); hTERT has non-telomere functions.
6. Blocking telomerase in cancer cells: antisense hTR in HeLa -> stop at 23-26 days; dominant-negative hTERT -> crisis after 5-25 days depending on initial telomere length (2-3 kb: immediately; 4-5 kb: ~30 days) (Hahn 1999); no effect before crisis (not toxic). Neuroblastoma: telomerase-negative tumors survive, positive do poorly; Ewing's sarcoma similar. Car analogy: oncogene = stuck accelerator, TSG = broken brakes, telomerase = endless fuel.
7. ALT (alternative lengthening): recombination between telomeres (marker spreads between telomeres), very long heterogeneous telomeres (>30 kb); ~half of osteosarcomas and soft-tissue sarcomas, 1/4 glioblastomas, rare carcinomas; dn hTERT doesn't affect ALT cells. Anti-telomerase drug would select ALT variants; ATR inhibitors kill ALT cells. Imetelstat-like 13-nt oligo inhibits 90-95%.
8. Mice: laboratory mouse telomeres 30-40 kb (5x human); mTERT not repressed; mouse cells immortalize spontaneously; human cells need large T + hTERT. mTR-/- mice normal for 3-4 generations; G5-G6 sick (poor wound healing, reduced fertility, atrophy of gut, blood, testes; premature aging) - telomeres shrink ~30 -> 25 kb per lifetime.
9. mTR-/- and cancer: with p16/p19ARF loss: tumors 64% (telomerase+) vs 31% (G4-5 mTR-/-); 16-week cancer deaths 88% vs 46% -> short telomeres SUPPRESS cancer when p53 intact. With p53 loss (p53+/-, mTR-/-): tumor rate INCREASES with generation (G1-2: ~50% by 24 weeks; G7-8: ~50% by 17 weeks, Chin 1999) and spectrum shifts from lymphomas/sarcomas to carcinomas (like humans). Model (Fig 10.23): p53 lost early -> telomere erosion -> BFB cycles survive -> aneuploidy, chromothripsis/kataegis -> telomerase or ALT stabilizes the scrambled karyotype.
   Humans: pancreatic progression: low-grade adenomas few anaphase bridges; high-grade dysplastic many; in situ carcinomas fewer again (a window). Short telomeres in esophageal, colon, breast carcinomas -> worse prognosis. Ulcerative colitis progressors: many anaphase bridges. Barrett's esophagus too. AML: 2/3 normal karyotype (longer telomeres) vs 1/3 scrambled (shorter). Hypomorphic hTERT allele (60% function) in ~1% of people, 3x overrepresented in AML. Dyskeratosis congenita -> MDS/AML, GI carcinomas. Telomeres shorten with age in lymphocytes/granulocytes (400 individuals); short lymphocyte telomeres -> more head-and-neck, bladder, lung, renal carcinomas. Baboon fibroblast TIFs increase with age.
10. Early embryo: ALT-like recombination lengthens telomeres in cleavage stage. hTERT non-canonical: protects against p53 apoptosis without catalysis; amplifies Wnt-b-catenin via Brg-1; mitochondria; TERRA lncRNA.
