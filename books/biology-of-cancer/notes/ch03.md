# Ch 3 Cancer as an Infectious Disease (book p.67-99; raw lines 10255-14214)

GIST: Tumor viruses turned cancer into a disease of genes. RSV transforms cells with ONE gene (src), whose continued
action is needed. That gene was stolen from the normal chicken genome: every vertebrate genome carries
proto-oncogenes that can be converted into oncogenes (by capture into a virus, or by a provirus inserted next to them).
Infectious agents still cause ~20% of lethal cancers worldwide (HBV/HCV, HPV, EBV, H. pylori), but not via these lab viruses.

1. Rous 1909-1911 (Rockefeller): chicken breast-muscle sarcoma ground in sand, filtered, filtrate injected -> sarcoma; serial passage; virus multiplies. RSV. Nobel only 1966. (1908 Danes: chicken leukemia filterable.)
2. Cell culture (Dulbecco lab, Temin & Rubin): RSV transforms chicken embryo fibroblasts in a dish -> cancer studiable at cell level.
   Normal cells stop at confluence (contact inhibition / density inhibition) -> monolayer. Transformed cells pile up -> focus visible by eye; a focus is a clone of one infected cell.
   Table 3.2 transformed-cell traits: altered morphology (rounded, refractile); loss of contact inhibition; anchorage independence (colonies in soft agar; good predictor of tumorigenicity); immortalization; reduced need for mitogenic growth factors (serum); high saturation density; cannot halt when GFs withdrawn; increased glucose import; glycolysis in presence of O2; tumorigenicity (acid test: syngeneic hosts or immunocompromised Nude mice).
3. 1970 Berkeley temperature-sensitive RSV mutant: transformed at 37C (permissive), reverts to normal at 41C, re-transforms at 37C many generations later -> viral protein needed continuously to MAINTAIN transformation (not hit-and-run); viral genome persists.
4. DNA tumor viruses: Shope papillomavirus (rabbit warts -> rare SCC); >100 HPV types; murine polyomavirus (1953); SV40 (40th simian virus; contaminated 1/3-2/3 of polio vaccines 1955-1963, 10-30 million exposed; epidemiology: little if any increased risk); adenovirus (hamster tumors); herpes: EBV (Burkitt's lymphoma in equatorial Africa/New Guinea; nasopharyngeal carcinoma SE Asia), KSHV/HHV-8; poxviruses (Shope fibroma, Yaba).
   Table 3.1 genome sizes (kb): HBV 3; SV40/polyoma 5; HPV16 8; adenovirus 5: 35; herpes (KSHV/EBV) 165; poxviruses 160. RNA: HTLV-I 8.2; RSV 9.3; HCV 9.6.
   Permissive cells (monkey for SV40): lytic, tens of thousands of progeny. Nonpermissive (rodent): no replication; rarely (1 in thousands) a transformant.
   Small genome -> only a few genes for transformation -> must act pleiotropically.
5. Persistence: T antigen (tumor antigen) in SV40 tumors; 1968 SV40 DNA co-sediments with >50 kb chromosomal DNA (alkaline sucrose gradient) -> covalently integrated; rare accident (<<1 per 1000 infections), haphazard, only early region needed.
   Cervical carcinoma: >99.7% carry integrated HPV fragments; E2 (repressor of E6/E7) lost in integration -> E6, E7 oncogenes expressed (ch8, 9).
   KSHV keeps episome tethered to chromatin; HHV-6/Marek's integrate into telomeres.
6. Retroviruses: RSV genome is ssRNA. Temin: provirus hypothesis (RNA -> dsDNA -> integrated -> transcribed). 1970 Temin & Baltimore: reverse transcriptase in virions. Integration obligatory and precise (integrase, pol gene). Genome: LTR-gag-pol-env-LTR (ALV); RSV = ALV + src at 3' end.
7. src is a cellular gene (Bishop & Varmus, UCSF, 1975-76): src probe = cDNA of wt RSV minus fragments that hybridize to transformation-defective (td) mutant RNA lacking src. Probe hybridized to UNINFECTED chicken DNA: two copies per diploid genome (single copy gene). Also in related birds, mammals; weaker with evolutionary distance (Fig 3.15, Spector/Varmus/Bishop PNAS 1978); later Drosophila and sponges (~550 My divergence) -> conserved normal gene = proto-oncogene c-src; v-src = kidnapped, remodeled.
   Capture model (Fig 3.16): ALV provirus integrates by chance next to c-src, co-transcribed, packaged -> RSV. Perhaps happened only once.
   Three implications: (a) other mutational mechanisms may activate proto-oncogenes in place; (b) one gene -> all transformation traits (pleiotropy), so maybe a few master genes suffice; (c) other retroviruses may carry other proto-oncogenes.
8. More acutely transforming retroviruses: MC29 (v-myc), feline sarcoma (fes), Harvey & Kirsten sarcoma viruses (H-ras, K-ras), Abelson (abl), simian sarcoma (sis = PDGF), kit, akt, jun, crk, erbB... Table 3.3. >50 vertebrate proto-oncogenes known, 30 found via retroviruses. All present in all vertebrates. Acquisition usually replaces viral replication genes -> defective virus needing helper.
9. Slow retroviruses (ALV, MLV; no oncogene) cause tumors after months: insertional mutagenesis. 1981: in >80% of ALV-induced leukemias/B-cell lymphomas, provirus integrated in or next to c-myc (mostly between noncoding exon 1 and exon 2, same orientation) -> viral promoter/enhancer drives myc unceasingly. Chance maybe 1 in 10 million infections -> slow. >25 proto-oncogenes found by integration sites. MMTV int-1 (= Wnt-1, homolog of fly wingless), int-2, int-3 in mouse mammary tumors. Gene therapy leukemias in children.
10. Third class: HTLV-I: viral tax gene (needed for replication) activates NF-kB & PI3K. ~1% of Kyushu inhabitants infected; ~1000 deaths/yr in Japan; 3-4% lifetime risk of adult T-cell leukemia; milk-borne mother->infant. Jaagsiekte sheep retrovirus env.
11. Infectious causes ~20% of lethal cancers worldwide: HBV/HCV liver (~600,000 deaths, via chronic inflammation), HPV (~610,000 deaths, viral oncoproteins).
    H. pylori: gastric carcinoma ~1 million cases (2012), 3/4 lethal, 3rd leading cancer death; >=75% due to H. pylori; Japan 99%. Cag pathogenicity island (40 kb, ~30 proteins) injects CagA via type IV secretion system -> polarity loss, Erk signaling, proliferation... Hit-and-run (not needed once tumor forms). Incubation >5 decades. Virulent alleles in East Asia.
