# Ch 16 Cancer Immunotherapy (book p.737-779; raw 95482-100594)

GIST: Three strategies. (1) Vaccination: prophylactic vaccines against cancer-causing infections work spectacularly
(HBV -> liver cancer; HPV -> cervical cancer, Gardasil 9 96.8% effective); therapeutic vaccines against existing tumors have
been weak (Sipuleucel-T: months). (2) Passive immunity: monoclonal antibodies kill by ADCC, phagocytosis, complement or by
blocking receptors (rituximab anti-CD20, trastuzumab anti-HER2); antibody-drug conjugates carry toxins (T-DM1,
sacituzumab); adoptive T-cell transfer of expanded tumor-infiltrating/neoantigen-reactive T cells; CAR T cells (antibody
binding domain + TCR signaling + co-stimulation, no MHC needed; anti-CD19 standard for B-ALL/B lymphoma; cytokine release
syndrome); bispecific T-cell engagers (blinatumomab CD3xCD19). (3) Releasing the brakes: checkpoint inhibitors anti-CTLA-4
(ipilimumab), anti-PD-1 (nivolumab, pembrolizumab), anti-PD-L1: CheckMate 067 melanoma 5-year survival 26%/44%/52% vs
historical 23%, with a survival plateau (cures); combination costs more toxicity (59% severe AEs). Problems: target shared with
normal cells (toxicity), antigen loss (11 of 16 CD19 CAR relapses), primary resistance (no preexisting T-cell response, IFN-g
pathway loss), immunosuppressive TME, T-cell exhaustion; personalized logistics; CAR-NK off-the-shelf promising.

Intro: infectious agents ~20% of cancers. Table 16.1: EBV, HTLV-1, HPV, KSHV/HHV8, MCPyV (direct, oncoproteins); HBV, HCV (indirect,
  inflammation); HIV (immunosuppression); H. pylori; flukes Opisthorchis viverrini, Schistosoma haematobium, Clonorchis sinensis.
16.1 Prophylactic vs therapeutic vaccines. Sidebar 16.1 Taiwan (Beasley): 22,707 government workers tested 1975; by 1986 HCC deaths 152 of 3454
  HBsAg+ vs 9 of 19,253 negative; relative risk 98.4 (book) (raw ratio (152/3454)/(9/19253) ~ 94). Liver cancer 25th cause of US cancer death.
16.2 HPV: >=99% of cervical cancers; ~4.5% of all cancer incidence worldwide; anus 88%, vulva 25%, vagina 78%, penis 50%; HNSCC fraction; US HPV
  oropharyngeal cancers now exceed cervical. >150 types, 12 oncogenic; HPV16+18 = 75% of HPV cancers (70% of cervical). Infection peaks in 20s,
  precancer ~10 years later, invasive cancer peak 40-50. L1 capsid virus-like particles (no DNA). Gardasil 9 (2014): types 6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33,
  45, 52, 58; 96.8% effective vs cervical cancer.
16.3 Therapeutic vaccines: direct (protein, synthetic long peptide, RNA, DNA) or antigen-loaded dendritic cells. mRNA melanoma vaccine (NY-ESO-1,
  MAGE-A3, TPTE, tyrosinase): 26% partial responses among 38 stage III/IV patients; antigen-specific CD8 from undetectable to ~10% of CD8s in 8 weeks.
  Sipuleucel-T (PAP-GM-CSF loaded monocytes/DCs): first FDA-approved therapeutic cancer vaccine; months of benefit. Driver vs passenger antigen matters.
16.4 Monoclonal antibodies: 5 killing modes (ADCC, antibody-dependent phagocytosis, complement MAC, complement C3b phagocytosis, receptor blockade).
  Naming (-ximab chimeric, -zumab humanized, -umab human); HAMA problem.
16.5 Rituximab anti-CD20: first mAb approved for cancer (1997); 85% of NHL are B-cell; R-CHOP beats CHOP in elderly DLBCL (Coiffier 2002).
  HER2+ 15-20% of breast cancers; trastuzumab meta-analysis 13,864 patients: -33% mortality with chemo. Pertuzumab blocks HER2-HER3 dimer (escape
  route); + docetaxel standard for HER2+ metastatic.
16.6 ADCs: antibody + linker + payload (pM-nM toxins); IgG1 half-life 21 days; bystander effect; Table 16.2 (gemtuzumab 2000 CD33 AML ... ). T-DM1
  beats lapatinib-capecitabine (EMILIA). TNBC ~15% of invasive breast cancers; sacituzumab govitecan (anti-Trop-2, SN-38) median OS 12.1 vs 6.7 months.
16.7 Adoptive cell transfer: TILs expanded with IL-2; lymphodepletion first; neoantigen minigenes (25-aa) on APCs select reactive T cells; expand
  to >10^10. Table 16.3 (Rosenberg): 2002 melanoma 13 patients 46% (lymphodepletion); 2008 NY-ESO-1 9 patients 33%; 2011 melanoma 93 patients 56%,
  20% complete >5 years; 2014 cervical 9 patients 33%; cholangiocarcinoma mutant ERBB2 1 patient >6 years; 2016 CRC KRAS G12D 1 patient >5 years;
  2018 breast 4 mutant proteins 1 patient. Labor-intensive, academic centers.
16.8 CAR T: scFv + hinge/transmembrane + CD3-zeta + co-stimulatory (CD28 or 4-1BB); no MHC, any surface antigen (protein, carbohydrate, lipid);
  autologous (GVH risk from native TCR); 2-3 weeks manufacture. Table 16.4 (Larson & Maus 2021): CD19 pediatric B-ALL (tisagenlecleucel, n=75,
  median age 11): 6-month relapse-free 80%, CRS 77%, neurotoxicity 40%; CD19 DLBCL (axi-cel, n=101, 58 y): ORR 83%, CR 58%, CRS 93%, neuro 67%;
  CD19 follicular (n=28): ORR 64%, CR 43%, CRS 57%, neuro 39%; CD19 mantle cell (n=68): ORR 93%, CR 67%, CRS 91%, neuro 63%; CD19 B-ALL CD28 (n=53):
  CR 83%, median OS 12.9 months, CRS 85%, neuro 44%; CD22 pre-B-ALL (n=21): CR 73% (higher dose), CRS 76%; BCMA myeloma ide-cel (n=33): ORR 85%, CR
  45%, CRS 76%, neuro 42%; BCMA (n=25): ORR 48%, CRS 88%, neuro 32%. CRS: IFN-g, IL-6, TNF, GM-CSF, IL-1; tocilizumab (anti-IL-6), anakinra,
  lenzilumab. Solid tumors lack clean antigens (B7-H3 in pediatric tumors). T cells cross blood-brain barrier (glioblastoma CARs).
16.9 Checkpoint inhibition: Leach, Krummel & Allison 1996: anti-CTLA-4 shrinks mouse colon tumors; anti-CD28 does the opposite. Anti-CTLA-4 also kills
  Tregs (they display CTLA-4). Iwai 2002: PD-L1 expression protects P815 mastocytoma cells from CTL killing; anti-PD-L1 restores. CTLA-4 acts in priming
  (lymph node); PD-1 in effector phase (tissue); PD-L1 induced by IFN-g/TNF (feedback). Table 16.5: ipilimumab (2011), anti-PD-1 (2014), anti-PD-L1
  (2016); in trials: LAG-3, TIM-3, PVRIG, VISTA, TIGIT, CD47/SIRPa.
16.10 CheckMate 067 (945 untreated stage III/IV melanoma; Larkin 2019): nivolumab+ipilimumab (n=314) / nivolumab (n=316) / ipilimumab (n=315):
  complete response 22% / 19% / 6%; partial 36% / 26% / 13%; total response 58% / 45% / 19%; high-grade adverse events 59% / 23% / 28%; 5-year
  overall survival 52% / 44% / 26% (historical ~23%); median treatment-free interval 18.1 / 1.8 / 1.9 months. Survival curves plateau from ~3 years
  (cures). High-mutation cancers more responsive (neoantigens). 2 Nobel prizes.
16.11 Resistance: ~half of melanoma patients unresponsive or transient; primary (no preexisting T-cell recognition, no antigen presentation, IFN-g
  receptor pathway loss) vs acquired; TME Tregs, M2, MDSCs, TGF-b.
16.12 BiTEs: blinatumomab (anti-CD3 x anti-CD19 scFvs); ALL: chemo CR 85-90% initially but relapse; salvage remissions 18-44% lasting 4-5 months;
  blinatumomab median 7.3 vs 4.6 months (overall survival, Kantarjian 2017); serious AEs 61% vs 45%; CRS, B-cell lymphopenia; resistance via antigen
  loss, PD-1, exhaustion.
16.13 Exhaustion: acute infection -> effector (die) + memory T cells; chronic antigen (persistent virus, cancer) -> exhausted (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3,
  TIGIT; no memory, low function); ex vivo IL-2 expansion pushes CAR/ACT cells toward exhaustion; "rest" cycles, CRISPR deletion of exhaustion TFs;
  anti-PD-1 reverses early exhaustion only.
16.14 Synopsis: shared-antigen toxicity (carcinomas ~80% of mortality; B lymphopenia tolerable); dual-antigen targeting (CD19/CD22 CARs) vs antigen
  loss (11 of 16 LBCL patients lost/lowered CD19 after CAR T); CAR-NK from cord blood: no TCR -> no GVH, off-the-shelf; 7 of 11 CD19 lymphoma/CLL
  patients no detectable tumor at 14 months, no CRS.
