# Ch 13 Dialogue Replaces Monologue: Heterotypic Interactions and the Biology of Angiogenesis (book p.549-607; raw 72100-78393)

GIST: A carcinoma is an organ, not a bag of cancer cells: stroma (fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, endothelial cells, pericytes,
macrophages, mast cells, lymphocytes, nerves, ECM) can be up to 90% of the cells, and the cancer cells keep depending on it
(heterotypic signaling: e.g., carcinoma PDGF -> stromal IGF back). Tumors get this stroma by switching on the normal
wound-healing program ("wounds that do not heal", Dvorak 1986): platelets/PDGF/TGF-b -> fibroblasts -> myofibroblasts,
macrophages, MMPs releasing ECM-bound growth factors, EMT at the wound edge, angiogenesis. Oxygen diffuses only ~0.2 mm, so a tumor
cannot grow beyond ~0.2 mm without blood vessels; the angiogenic switch (Rip-Tag: mast cells/macrophages release MMP-9 which frees
ECM-bound VEGF) is a separate step of progression = another barrier breached. Tumor vessels are chaotic, leaky (10x), wide (3x),
poorly covered by pericytes; no working lymphatics -> high interstitial pressure blocks drug delivery. Anti-angiogenesis targets
genetically stable normal cells (should not select resistance) but tumors switch factors (FGF), co-opt vessels, and hypoxia
(HIF-1) makes them more aggressive; Avastin extends progression-free survival by months, rarely overall survival.

Intro: carcinomas >80% of cancer burden. Paraneoplastic syndromes (endocrine).
13.1 Stroma up to 90% of cells (breast, colon, stomach, pancreas carcinomas); Hodgkin's: up to 99% non-neoplastic (Reed-Sternberg);
  hemangioma mostly neoplastic. scRNAseq pancreatic adenocarcinoma 57,530 cells: ductal 1 6.30%, ductal 2 26.95%, acinar 1.22%, endocrine 1.09%,
  endothelial 12.23%, fibroblast 13.82%, stellate 12.59%, macrophage 11.44%, T 8.61%, B 5.75% (from Peng 2019; counts 2646, 11315, 512, 459, 5134, 5802,
  5282, 4802, 3616, 2416). Heterotypic ligand-receptor pairs: PDGF epithelium -> PDGFR mesenchyme; HGF mesenchyme -> Met epithelium; FGF10 ->
  FGFR2b; Jagged/Notch. TGF-b acts on almost every stromal cell type. Van Scott & Reinertson 1961: autografted basal cell carcinomas grew only
  with their stroma (1-5 weeks). Carcinoma PDGF -> stromal IGF-1; melanoma PDGF -> fibroblast IGF-2; breast stroma SDF-1/CXCL12 + HGF.
  Pericytes: recruited by endothelial PDGF/HB-EGF, return VEGF + Ang-1. Metastases rebuild stroma. Melanoma brain metastases recruit T cells;
  gliomas don't (cancer cells set the TME). Ascites spheroids nearly stroma-independent. Cell lines select stroma independence; xenograft
  response poorly predictive (2001 study of 39 drugs: >1/3 xenografts responding -> ~50% chance of any clinical activity).
13.2 ECM: basement membrane (laminin, collagen IV, perlecan, nidogen); ECM up to 25% of carcinoma mass; up to 90% in pancreatic ductal
  adenocarcinoma. ~300 core matrisome proteins. ECM = growth-factor reservoir ("message board"). 3D hydrogel with breast ECM: normal MECs build
  ducts/lobules without stroma; ~5% ER/PR+. Culture conditions (Fig 13.11): tissue O2 0-5% vs dish 21%; stiffness 200-4000 Pa vs 3x10^9 Pa plastic;
  pH acidic <7 vs 7.2; 3D vs 2D; glucose limiting vs not; stroma present vs absent; human vs bovine GFs. PDX better but slow.
13.3 Wound healing: platelets release PDGF, TGF-b; fibrin clot; fibroblasts -> myofibroblasts (TGF-b; alpha-SMA; contract wound);
  MMPs (zinc) degrade ECM, free bFGF, TGF-b1, PDGF, EGFs, IFN-g; macrophages, neutrophils; VEGF/FGF -> angiogenesis; epithelial edge cells EMT
  (E-cadherin down, N-cadherin up, vimentin up), migrate, then MET. Table 13.1 MMPs. Sidebar 13.4: breast cancer recurrence peak ~3 years after
  surgery; wound fluid mitogenic; Herceptin post-op cuts relapse up to 50%. Tumor capillaries leaky -> fibrinogen -> fibrin (Tissue Factor).
  Carcinoma cells release PDGF continuously (wound: brief burst). High stroma:epithelium ratio = poor prognosis. CAFs: alpha-SMA+ myCAFs near
  cancer cells (collagen I, desmoplasia; pancreatic tumors 100x harder than glioblastomas) vs IL-6-secreting iCAFs further away. Origins: local
  fibroblasts + TGF-b; bone-marrow MSCs/fibrocytes. Core serum response (CSR) signature of serum-stimulated fibroblasts predicts metastasis
  (stage 1 breast) and death (lung adenocarcinoma) (Chang 2004).
13.4 Experiments: PDGF-secreting keratinocytes (no PDGFR) become tumorigenic in vivo only (stroma recruited). Elenbaas 2001: transformed HMECs
  (SV40 ER + hTERT + weak ras) form tumors slowly in ~half of mice; + normal breast fibroblasts -> rapid, 100% (1/3 of the time). Olumi 1999:
  SV40T-immortalized prostate epithelial cells alone ~10 mg; + CAFs median 10x larger (text: up to 500x); + normal prostate fibroblasts ~7 mg;
  CAFs + normal epithelium ~10 mg. Orimo 2005: CAFs recruit endothelial progenitors via SDF-1/CXCL12 (anti-SDF-1 cuts recruitment 60%) and
  make VEGF. Glioblastoma stem cells -> endothelial cells (2010); vasculogenic mimicry (VE-cadherin).
13.5 Macrophages: recruited by MCP-1/CCL2, CSF-1, VEGF, PDGF. MMTV-PyMT Csf1 op/op: primary tumor grows but invasion/metastasis suppressed (Lin 2001).
  TAM density correlates with microvessel density and poor prognosis (glioma; breast, ovary, prostate, cervix, bladder, lung). TAMs make VEGF,
  IL-8, MMP-9 (frees ECM factors; cleaves IGFBPs -> IGF-1), EGF (breast). M1 vs M2. Immature myeloid cells (Apc/Smad4 mice: CCL9 -> CCR1+ iMCs
  with MMP-2/9 at invasive front; CCR1 KO -> invasion depth 10x less). MDSCs.
13.6 O2 diffusion limit ~0.2 mm; necrosis begins 85 um (melanoma) and 110 um (prostate) from capillary; hypoxia, low pH. Corneal/ear implants
  attract vessels (Folkman). Tumor capillaries 3x wider; chaotic, dead ends; Ang-1/Ang-2 imbalance; VEGF-A overexpression alone mimics.
  Endothelial gaps several um; tumor vessel walls ~10x more permeable; VEGF = VPF (1979/1989). WNT-medulloblastoma: fenestrated vessels, up to
  300x drug transport -> curable. Lymphatics collapse inside tumors (VEGF-C/D, VEGFR3); interstitial pressure high (plus PDGF-induced
  myofibroblast contraction) -> poor drug delivery.
13.7 Angiogenic switch: Rip-Tag (insulin promoter -> SV40 large T + small t in beta cells); ~400 islets; ~half hyperplastic by 5-10 weeks;
  8-12% (~10%) of hyperplastic become angiogenic (7-12 weeks); 2-4% (~3%) of initial hyperplastic become invasive carcinomas by 12-14 weeks.
  Pre-angiogenic nests 0.1-0.2 mm: proliferation balanced by apoptosis (hypoxia). Beta cells make VEGF all along but it is ECM-bound; MMP-9
  from mast cells/macrophages releases it. VEGF deletion in islets: no switch. Kit-/- (no mast cells) Rip-Tag: tumors stay 0.1-0.2 mm; wild-type
  bone marrow transplant restores angiogenesis. Some tumors: rheostat not switch; transformed fibroblasts use FGFs etc. (Table 13.3 factors).
13.8 Angiogenesis starts before basement membrane breach (DCIS, K14-HPV16 skin) but intensifies with invasion. Microvessel density predicts
  breast cancer disease-free survival over 20 years (Heimann 1998); node+ breast: HER2-VEGF- >80% alive at 8 years vs HER2+VEGF+ ~35% (Konecny 2004).
  Clonal heterogeneity in angiogenic power (liposarcoma clones: microvessel density vs tumor volume). VEGF mobilizes bone-marrow EPCs;
  Id1+/- Id3-/- mice can't grow LLC tumors; rescued by wild-type bone marrow (Lyden 2001).
13.9 Anti-angiogenesis: target normal, genetically stable cells -> less resistance (theory). Tumor endothelial cells live ~1 week vs normal
  hundreds of days to 7 years. Anti-VEGF antibody 1993; Avastin (bevacizumab) + 5-FU: +4 months metastatic CRC; +2 months NSCLC; RCC slowed,
  no long-term survival; 3.5 million patients by 2020; mostly PFS not OS. Rip-Tag: SU5416 (VEGFR2 TKI) blocked 90% of switches but not late
  tumors; SU6668 (PDGFR, pericytes) halved switches but halved late tumor size; combination >98% held pre-angiogenic, 90% block of small tumor
  expansion, ~80-85% regression of large tumors. Both drugs failed in clinic. Anti-VEGF alone: vessels regrow in pericyte/BM sleeves.
  Sidebar 13.9: anti-VEGFR2 Rip-Tag tumors regress >50% then relapse via FGFs. Table 13.4 resistance (vascular mimicry, alternative factors,
  p53-null hypoxia tolerance, mature vessels, BM cells). Table 13.5 approved drugs.
13.10 Nerves: perineural invasion (1905); denervation shrinks prostate xenografts/transgenic tumors; nerve density may double; tumor cells
  induce neurites; glutamate-fed brain metastases.
13.11 Synopsis: interdependence imposes regimentation. ~95% of tumors recognizable histologically; ~5% anaplastic. Sidebar 13.10: small numbers
  of malignant human breast cancer cells mixed with normal mouse MECs join normal ducts, no tumors. Hypoxia paradox: hypoxic tumors worse; HIF-1
  induces VEGF, PDGF, TGF-a/b, MMPs, Met (HGF receptor) -> EMT (Twist, Snail up). Vessel normalization idea. Tumor stroma ~12 cell types
  ("three-body problem"). Therapy targets map (Fig 13.45).
