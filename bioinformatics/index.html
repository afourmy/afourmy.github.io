Bioinformatics

Notes on Bioinformatics Algorithms by Phillip Compeau and Pavel Pevzner.

Chapter 1: Replication Origins
Where on a bacterial chromosome does copying begin? Frequent k-mers, clumps, approximate matching, and the running count of G minus C that locates the origin of Escherichia coli to within 150 nucleotides out of four and a half million.

Chapter 2: Motif Finding
A regulatory pattern is shared by a collection of DNA regions but occurs nowhere exactly. Scoring a set of motifs, the median string, greedy search, and the randomized algorithms that beat it.

Chapter 3: Genome Assembly
No machine reads a chromosome end to end. Rebuilding one from short reads is a Hamiltonian path problem until the reads are moved from the nodes of the graph onto its edges, at which point it becomes an Eulerian path problem and is solved in linear time.

Chapter 4: Antibiotic Sequencing
Some antibiotics are assembled by an enzyme rather than transcribed from a gene, so no gene spells them out. Their sequence has to be recovered from a list of fragment masses, by branch and bound, a leaderboard, and the spectral convolution.

Chapter 5: Sequence Alignment
Comparing two sequences is finding a longest path in a directed acyclic graph. Global, local, fitting and overlap alignment are one algorithm with different ends freed, and the chapter ends by finding the homeobox shared by a fly gene and a human one.

Chapter 6: Genome Rearrangements
Two genomes too far apart to compare letter by letter still share whole blocks in a different order. Reversals, breakpoints, and the graph in which a reversal, a translocation, a fusion and a fission all turn out to be one operation, which makes the distance a subtraction.

Chapter 7: Evolutionary Trees
Reconstructing who descended from whom, first from a matrix of pairwise distances and then from the sequences themselves, and using it to name the animal that gave us SARS.

Chapter 8: Gene Clustering
Yeast ferments sugar into alcohol even when it could burn it, and switches to burning the alcohol only once the sugar is gone. Partitioning genes by how their activity moves, with k-centres, k-means, expectation maximization and a dendrogram, reads that switch off the data.

Chapter 9: Read Mapping
Finding a patient's disease-causing mutation means placing a billion short reads on a reference genome. Tries, suffix trees and suffix arrays each answer the question and each one runs out of memory, until the Burrows-Wheeler transform shrinks the index to something that fits and locates a pathogenic variant in KAT6B.

Chapter 10: Hidden Markov Models
A machine that emits symbols while moving between states nobody can see. Recovering what it was doing from what it produced finds CG-islands on human chromosome X, and a model of a whole family of sequences rediscovers the consensus of the HIV envelope V3 loop and tells its members from everything else.
