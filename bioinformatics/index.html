Bioinformatics

Notes on Bioinformatics Algorithms by Phillip Compeau and Pavel Pevzner.

Chapter 1: Replication Origins
Where on a bacterial chromosome does copying begin? Frequent k-mers, clumps, approximate matching, and the running count of G minus C that locates the origin of Escherichia coli to within 150 nucleotides out of four and a half million.

Chapter 2: Motif Finding
A regulatory pattern is shared by a collection of DNA regions but occurs nowhere exactly. Scoring a set of motifs, the median string, greedy search, and the randomized algorithms that beat it.

Chapter 3: Genome Assembly
No machine reads a chromosome end to end. Rebuilding one from short reads is a Hamiltonian path problem until the reads are moved from the nodes of the graph onto its edges, at which point it becomes an Eulerian path problem and is solved in linear time.

Chapter 4: Antibiotic Sequencing
Some antibiotics are assembled by an enzyme rather than transcribed from a gene, so no gene spells them out. Their sequence has to be recovered from a list of fragment masses, by branch and bound, a leaderboard, and the spectral convolution.

Chapter 5: Sequence Alignment
Comparing two sequences is finding a longest path in a directed acyclic graph. Global, local, fitting and overlap alignment are one algorithm with different ends freed, and the chapter ends by finding the homeobox shared by a fly gene and a human one.
